# Patterns - Preludes and codas

**Prelude** (before shared context; introduces themes vividly):
- Quotation: "If you're old enough to fight for your country, you're old enough to drink to it."
- Startling fact: three of four students binged in the past thirty days; the heaviest are fraternity officers.
- Illustrative anecdote: Jim S., fraternity president, dies from a dare.
- Combined: quotation > anecdote > fact, weaving a recurring figure (Jim S.) through, then shared context ("Drinking has been part of American college life...") > *But* problem.

**Coda:** end with anecdote/quotation/fact echoing the prelude.

Genre: rare in natural/social sciences, more in humanities, most in general-public writing. A prelude is optional and never replaces the problem.
