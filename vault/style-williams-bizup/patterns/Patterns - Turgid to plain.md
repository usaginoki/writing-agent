# Patterns - Turgid to plain

| Shape (before) | Fix (after) |
|---|---|
| Abstract noun subject + "could lead to" + nominal object: *An understanding of the causal factors involved in excessive drinking by students could lead to their more effective treatment.* | People as subjects, real verbs: *We could more effectively treat students who drink excessively if we understood why they do so.* |
| *Recognition of the fact that systems differ ... can serve as the basis for serious consideration of ...* (nominal chain, ~2x length) | *When we recognize that languages have different grammars, we can consider the problems of those who translate ...* |
| Cooper: *The love of turgid expressions is gaining ground, and ought to be corrected* ... (abstract, indirect) | *We should discourage those who promote turgid language. A well-bred person speaks simply ...* |
| Orwell: *The keynote is the elimination of simple verbs. Instead of being a single word ... a verb becomes a phrase* | *Pretentious writers avoid simple verbs. Instead of using one word ... they turn the verb into a noun or adjective ...* |
| "verb phrase" = noun/adjective + prove/serve/form/play/render | single verb (break, stop, kill, spoil, mend) |
| *by examination of* | *by examining* |

**Method:** find the real actors and actions; make actors subjects and actions verbs; cut filler. Even critics of turgid style slip into it, so check your own.
