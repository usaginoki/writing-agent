# Style: Lessons in Clarity and Grace (Williams & Bizup), Index

**The core idea:** readers judge prose by how it feels to read, and those feelings come from specific, diagnosable features of sentences, paragraphs, and documents. The principles are **revision tools, not drafting rules** ([[Draft first then revise with principles]]). Writers can't trust their own sense of clarity, because they read their intent into the page ([[Judge your prose as readers experience it]]). So you diagnose mechanically, then revise.

**The book's two governing metaphors:**
- **Story:** characters are subjects, actions are verbs.
- **Information flow:** old before new, short before long, point before detail.

## The book's Ten Principles for Writing Clearly
1. Distinguish real rules from folklore: [[Do not enforce folklore rules]] · [[Patterns - Folklore rules]]
2. Use subjects to name the characters in your story: [[Make main characters subjects]]
3. Use verbs to name their important actions: [[Make important actions verbs]] · [[Turn nominalizations into verbs]]
4. Open sentences with familiar units of information: [[Begin sentences with old information]]
5. Get to the main verb quickly:
   - avoid long introductions: [[Get to the subject of the main clause quickly]]
   - avoid long abstract subjects: [[Shorten long abstract subjects]]
   - avoid interrupting subject and verb: [[Do not interrupt subject and verb]]
6. Push new, complex information to the end: [[End sentences with new information]] · [[Introduce technical terms in stress position]]
7. Begin sentences in a unit with consistent subjects/topics: [[Keep topic strings short and consistent]]
8. Be concise:
   - cut meaningless and repeated words and obvious implications: [[Delete meaningless words]] · [[Delete obvious implications]]
   - turn phrases into words: [[Replace a phrase with a word]]
   - prefer affirmatives to negatives: [[Change negatives to affirmatives]]
9. Control sprawl:
   - don't chain subordinate clauses: [[Do not chain subordinate clauses after the main clause]]
   - extend sentences with resumptive, summative and free modifiers: [[Extend sentences with resumptive, summative, and free modifiers]]
   - extend with coordination after the verb: [[Extend sentences with coordination]]
10. Write to others as you would have others write to you: [[Write to others as you would have them write to you]]

## Revision order: top-down
The book spends revision effort on beginnings first ([[Spend revision effort on beginnings]]). The order is document → section → paragraph → sentence, then grace.

### 1. Document: motivation and global coherence (L07, L08)
