# L04 Characters

**Summary:** Readers want actions in verbs but want characters as subjects even more; deleting characters (`There was fear...`) is worst. Diagnose by looking at subjects, then find hidden characters in possessives, prepositional objects and adjectives. Abstractions can be virtual characters if familiar and consistent. The passive is not banned: choose by agent relevance, old-new flow and point of view. Academic writers do use `I/we`, with metadiscourse verbs; research procedures are commonly passive. Avoid invented noun strings. Professional voice should be as complex as necessary and no more.

## Principles
- [[Name characters in subjects instead of deleting them]]
- [[Recover characters hidden in possessives, prepositions and adjectives]]
- [[Make abstractions virtual characters only when familiar]]
- [[Choose active or passive by three questions]]
- [[Use the passive when the agent is unknown or irrelevant]]
- [[Use the passive to put familiar information first]]
- [[Hold one point of view across sentences]]
- [[Use I and we with metadiscourse verbs]]
- [[Unpack noun strings]]
- [[Be as complex as necessary and no more]]
- (core, from L03) [[Make main characters subjects]]

## Summing up (condensed)
1. Clear prose: subjects name characters, verbs name actions.
2. If abstract nominalizations are your characters, use as few other nominalizations as possible.
3. Passive if agent self-evident; 4. if it replaces a long subject with a short one; 5. if it yields a coherent sequence of subjects.
6. Active if it is a metadiscourse verb. 7. Rewrite long compound noun phrases.

## Also covered
Reader strategy for dense text: retell as character-action pairs. Professional voice: groups expect a distinctive voice, but needless density is exclusionary; Writer's Golden Rule: write to others as you would have others write to you. Dangling modifier from procedural passive is accepted scientific usage.
