# L03 Actions

**Summary:** Words like *clear, dense, abstract* describe how readers feel, not what is in the sentence. Readers feel a sentence is clear when main characters are its subjects and their important actions are its verbs. Nominalizations (abstract nouns derived from verbs/adjectives), especially as subjects of empty verbs, make prose abstract and indirect. A diagnose-analyze-rewrite routine converts them, with five common patterns. Benefits: concrete, concise, logical, coherent. But some nominalizations are useful. Clarity does not mean simple Dick-and-Jane sentences.

## Principles
- [[Make main characters subjects]] — characters should be simple subjects

- [[Link revised clauses with precise connectors]] — because/if/although instead of of/by

## Summing up (condensed)
1. Make main characters subjects; make their important actions verbs. Readers integrate a fixed grammatical level (subject, verb) with a variable story level (character, action) and want them to match.
2. Characters must be *the* subject, not merely in it (`president's veto` -> `the president vetoed`).
3. Express actions in verbs; make associated characters their subjects.
4. Do not revise nominalizations that refer to a previous sentence, replace `The fact that`, name an object, or name a familiar concept (virtual character).

## Happy consequences
More concrete; more concise (fewer articles/prepositions); clearer logic (conjunctions); more coherent story (order of actions).

## Quotes
