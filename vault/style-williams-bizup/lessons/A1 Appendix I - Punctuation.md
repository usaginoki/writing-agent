# A1 Appendix I - Punctuation

**Summary:** Punctuation is mostly choice, not rule. Distinguish punctuated sentences (capital to period) from grammatical sentences (main clause + dependents): the same grammar punctuated differently feels different. Always signal sentence ends (ten ways: three conventional, four debatable, three stylish); a few firm rules govern beginnings and middles; coordinated elements and series follow tendencies with stylistic exceptions. Write a well-structured sentence and the punctuation largely takes care of itself.

## Principles
- [[Signal the end of every grammatical sentence]] - rule; avoid run-ons
- [[Use a semicolon only between short linked sentences]] - soft period; *however* fix
- [[Join sentences with a comma and a coordinating conjunction]] - conventional join
- [[End a sentence with a colon, dash or parenthesis for effect]] - stylish
- [[Use a colon only after a complete clause]] - rule
- [[Use sentence fragments only on purpose]] - choice
- [[Follow the five rules for commas after introductory elements]] - rules
- [[Use a comma after a long introductory element and skip it after a short one]] - principle
- [[Never put a single comma between subject and verb]] - rule
- [[Set off long interruptions with paired punctuation]] - rule
- [[Set off nonrestrictive modifiers with commas]] - rule
- [[Use dashes and parentheses for loose commentary]] - choice
- [[Do not put a comma between two coordinated elements]] - default + 4 exceptions
- [[Punctuate a series with commas or semicolons]] - tendency
- [[Use apostrophes only for contractions and possessives]] - rules

## Summing up (condensed)
- Always signal the end of a grammatical sentence.
- Always observe the five reliable rules (pp. 217-218).
- Always set off long interrupting elements with commas.
- Never put a single comma between subject and verb or verb and object.
- Otherwise punctuate to show readers the connections and separations they need; write a clear sentence first.

## Notable quotes
> "punctuation is not governed by rules, but by choices" (p. 209)
