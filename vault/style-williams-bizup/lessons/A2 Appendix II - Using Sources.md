# A2 Appendix II - Using Sources

**Summary:** Using sources properly protects your ethos. Plagiarism is theft of words and credit, and readers judge words, not intentions. Cite quoted words, paraphrases and borrowed ideas; take disciplined notes; paraphrase in new structure; weave quotations into your own argument rather than dropping them in; format by length; punctuate quotations correctly; use a standard citation style.

## Principles
- [[Cite every word and idea you borrow]]
- [[Take notes that keep source words visibly separate]]
- [[Paraphrase in a new structure, not with synonyms]]
- [[Weave quotations into your own sentences]]
- [[Introduce each quotation with a signal phrase that fits it]]
- [[Format quotations by length]]
- [[Quote striking words once]]
- [[Punctuate quotations correctly]]
- [[Use one standard citation style]]
- [[Keep your voice dominant]]

## Summing up (condensed)
1. Credit a source whenever you use its words or ideas (quote + page, paraphrase + page, idea/method + citation).
2. Make each quotation fit: indicate its relation first; weave not drop; run in up to four lines, block five or more; quote distinctive words at first use.
3. Use a standard citation style (Chicago, MLA, APA).
Chain of thinking = chain of trust.

## Notable quotes
> "avoid anything that might lead an informed reader to think that you are taking credit for words or ideas not your own" (p. 231)
