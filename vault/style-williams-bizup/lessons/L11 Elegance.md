# L11 Elegance

**Summary:** Clear, concise, coherent prose can still be dry. A few devices add grace: balance and symmetry (coordinated and not), climactic endings (weighty words, *of* + weighty word, echoing salience, chiasmus, suspension), and deliberate sentence length. Knowing the devices does not make you elegant; reading elegant writers and imitating them does. Use them sparingly; "less is more." Part 4 summary adds: try balancing parts of sentences, especially their last few words.

## Principles
- [[Balance coordinated parts of a sentence]] - echo in order, sound, meaning; longer second parts
- [[Balance uncoordinated parts of a sentence]] - subject vs. object, clause vs. clause
- [[End sentences on weighty words]] - nouns over prepositions
- [[End with of plus a weighty word]] - light run-up, strong close
- [[Echo an earlier word at the sentence end]] - echoing salience
- [[Use chiasmus for special effect]] - reverse the order in the second half
- [[Suspend the climax with parallel openers]] - delay the point for drama
- [[Vary sentence length only at the extremes]] - below ~15 or above ~30 for all
- [[Build elegance on simple subjects and verbs]] - clarity base
- [[Use elegant devices sparingly]] - accents, not style
- [[Learn balance by imitating models]] - exercises 11.1-11.4

## Summing up
Elegant passages combine (1) simple characters as subjects and actions as verbs, (2) complex balance of syntax, meaning, sound and rhythm, (3) artfully stressed endings. Read the elegant until the style "runs along your muscles and nerves"; only then can you tell elegant from inflated. The reliable rule is *Less is more*.
