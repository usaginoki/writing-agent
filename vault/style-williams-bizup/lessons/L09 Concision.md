# L09 Concision

**Summary:** Readers judge prose by length as well as structure. Six principles trim words that mean nothing, repeat, are implied, could be one word, are negative, or are useless modifiers. Metadiscourse, hedges and intensifiers are needed but must be moderated. Concision must not tip into terseness; ask readers.

## Principles
- [[Delete meaningless words]]: cut tics like *actually, really, certain*
- [[Delete redundant pairs]]: *each and every*
- [[Delete redundant modifiers]]: *final outcome*
- [[Delete redundant categories]]: *pink in color*
- [[Delete obvious implications]]: diffuse redundancy
- [[Replace a phrase with a word]]: *despite the fact that* to *although*
- [[Change negatives to affirmatives]] and [[Untangle stacked negatives]]
- [[Delete useless adjectives and adverbs]]
- [[Cut metadiscourse that attributes ideas to a source]]
- [[Cut metadiscourse that announces your topic]]
- [[Work topic phrases into the subject]]
- [[Calibrate hedges]] and [[Calibrate intensifiers]]
- [[Don't announce your point; state it]]
- [[Be concise, not terse]]

## Summing Up (condensed)
Clear deadwood to see the sentence's shape: 1 meaningless words; 2 redundant pairs; 3 redundant modifiers; 4 redundant categories; 5 obvious implications; 6 a phrase for a word; 7 indirect negatives; 8 excessive metadiscourse; 9 hedges and intensifiers (Goldilocks: not too much, not too little; trust your ear). Example: "It is almost certainly the case that totalitarian systems cannot allow a society to have what we would define as stable social relationships." becomes "Totalitarianism prevents stable social relationships."
