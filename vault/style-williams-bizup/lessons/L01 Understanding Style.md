# L01 Understanding Style

**Summary.** The book assumes clear writing is good and that anyone can learn it. Unclear writing is a 450-year-old social habit (bureaucratese, legalese, academese) and also has private causes: wanting to look deep, hiding ignorance, fear of error, freezing in a new field, and above all not knowing how one's own prose reads to others. Writers read their thoughts into their words, so they cannot judge their own clarity unaided. The cure is to learn how readers experience words on the page, draft fast, then revise with principles (not rules). Clarity is not the only goal (Kennedy-style occasions want more: Part Four), and style has an ethical side (Lesson 12).

## Principles
- [[Draft first then revise with principles]] - principles are revision tools, not drafting rules.
- [[Judge your prose as readers experience it]] - you read your intent into the page; test from the reader's side.
- [[Do not inflate prose to seem deep]] - complexity is not depth; imitating dense prose compounds confusion.
- [[Use the principles to mentally revise difficult reading]] - separate necessary from gratuitous complexity in others' prose.

## Summing up (condensed)
- Clarity is learnable; unclear writing has social history and private causes.
- Biggest cause: not knowing when/why readers will find you unclear.
- Don't apply principles while drafting; use them to spot and fix problem sentences.
- Rules do not make writers; principles that explain reader response can.
