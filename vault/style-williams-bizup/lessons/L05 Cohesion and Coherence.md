# L05 Cohesion and Coherence

**Summary:** Clear individual sentences don't make a passage feel connected. Readers judge [[Cohesion]] by how each sentence ends and the next begins, and [[Coherence]] by how all sentences cumulatively begin. Cohesion comes from old-before-new ordering (the passive exists for this); coherence comes from short, consistent topics as subjects. Don't fake either with conjunctions; don't vary subjects for variety.

## Principles
- [[Begin sentences with old information]] - familiar first
- [[End sentences with new information]] - unpredictable last
- [[Link the end of one sentence to the start of the next]] - the flow handoff
- [[Use the passive to put old information first]] - why the passive exists
- [[Favor cohesion over single-sentence clarity]] - priority rule
- [[Put reference signals at the start of the sentence]] - this/another/such up front
- [[Make subjects name topics]] - subject ≠ always topic
- [[Keep topic strings short and consistent]] - passage-level focus
- [[Clear throat-clearing before the subject]] - no pile-ups before topic
- [[Do not vary subjects for variety]] - monotony is mostly the writer's illusion
- [[Do not fake coherence with conjunctions]] - connectors must match real logic

## Summing Up (condensed)
1. Begin sentences with subjects that carry old information; put new, unfamiliar information toward the end.
2. Through a series of sentences keep topics short and reasonably consistent.
Framework: Topic slot = familiar (variable) / Subject = character; the lesson fills "Topic: familiar-new" into the subject-verb frame.

Other points: skim a hard text first for its main characters (Quick Tip); list characters before drafting a section; the Sayner/mashed-potatoes passage is cohesive yet incoherent; changing which character is subject shifts point of view (Ex. 5.3).
