# L06 Emphasis

**Summary:** The end of a sentence is its [[Stress]] position. Readers handle complexity best late, so open simple and short and end with long, complex, new material, especially technical terms. Use the end for emphasis by trimming, shifting peripheral material left, new information right, and syntactic shifts. In opening sentences, stress the key [[Theme]]s that the passage will thread, which boosts [[Coherence]].

## Principles
- [[Open sentences with short simple elements]] - simplicity to complexity
- [[End sentences with new information]] - (shared with L05)
- [[Introduce technical terms in stress position]]
- [[Put the words you want emphasized in stress position]]
- [[Trim the end of the sentence]]
- [[Shift peripheral ideas to the left]]
- [[Shift new information to the right]]
- [[Use there, what, and it shifts to control stress]]
- [[Use the passive to put old information first]] - also a stress device
- [[Use not only-but to stress the second element]]
- [[Avoid ending a sentence on a repeated word]]
- [[Announce key themes in the stress of the opening sentence]]
- [[Thread themes through a passage]]

## Summing Up (condensed)
1. Use the end of a sentence for long, complex, difficult material, esp. unfamiliar technical terms and new information.
2. Use the very end to emphasize words you want heard.
3. Use the stress of an introductory sentence to announce the key themes of the passage.
Part 2 summary, readers want: (1) quick arrival at a main-clause subject; (2) a quick subject-to-verb: short, concrete, ideally character subjects with familiar information first; (3) verbs naming specific actions (no buried nominalizations); (4) the least familiar, most complex material at the end; (5) a few consistent topics through a passage; (6) themes threaded through. Apply these in subordinate clauses too.

Framework: Topic (short, simple, familiar) ... Stress (long, complex, new); Subject = character; Verb = action.

## Notable quotes
> "carry the reader not from complexity to simplicity, but from simplicity to complexity." (p. 83)
> Themes: "locate at the end of an introductory sentence words that announce the key themes." (p. 90)
