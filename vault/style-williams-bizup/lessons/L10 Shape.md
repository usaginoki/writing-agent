# L10 Shape

**Summary:** Writers need to write sentences longer than about twenty words that are still clear; length alone does not make a sentence ungainly, sprawl does ([[Sprawl]]). Readers sense shapeless length when they wait too long for the main verb, slog through tacked-on subordinate clauses after it, or are stopped by interruptions. So: get quickly to subject, verb and object; open with the point; avoid chains of subordinate clauses and extend sentences with resumptive, summative, and free modifiers or with well-ordered, parallel coordination; then troubleshoot long sentences for faulty coordination, ambiguous or dangling modifiers. Underlying logic: short-before-complex (short point, short subject, old before new, short before long coordinate).

## Principles
- [[Get to the subject of the main clause quickly]] — no long openers; periodic exception
- [[Shorten long abstract subjects]] — subject > 7-8 words, nominalizations, relative clauses
- [[Do not interrupt subject and verb]]
- [[Do not interrupt verb and object]]
- [[Open a long sentence with its point]] — point in a short main clause first
- [[Do not chain subordinate clauses after the main clause]] — cut, split, modifiers, coordinate
- [[Convert although and since clauses into independent clauses]]
- [[Extend sentences with resumptive, summative, and free modifiers]]
- [[Extend sentences with coordination]]
- [[Order coordinate elements from short to long]]
- [[Coordinate only elements parallel in grammar and sense]]
- [[Keep long coordinations traceable]]
- [[Use correlative conjunctions before parallel parts]]
- [[Place modifiers where they are unambiguous]]
- [[Give dangling modifiers an explicit subject]]

## Summing up (condensed)
1. Reach subject, then verb and object, fast: revise long openers into independent clauses; turn long subjects into introductory clauses (move to the end or split if long); move interruptions to the beginning or end.
2. Open with the point in a short main clause.
3. After the main clause, avoid subordinate-on-subordinate: trim relatives, split, use resumptive/summative/free modifiers, coordinate parallel elements.
Punctuation helps long sentences (Appendix I).
