# L12 The Ethics of Style

**Summary:** Subject and verb choices imply philosophies of action and affect honesty. Writers owe readers clarity; readers owe effort on genuinely hard ideas. The First Rule of Ethical Writing: write to others as you would have them write to you. Obscurity may be unintended (fix, respond candidly) or intended (Sears ad, gas rate notice, recall letter: hide agents, nominalize and passivize danger). Claims that new ideas require difficult prose, that difficulty improves thought, or that clarity is oppressive are mostly wrong. Jefferson's Declaration shows grammar arguing silently: agent in Part 2, colonists as agents in Part 3, abstractions coercing in Part 1.

## Principles
- [[Write to others as you would have them write to you]]
- [[Make ideas no simpler than they deserve and no harder than necessary]]
- [[Earn a reliable ethos by helping readers]]
- [[Do not hide the responsible agent to deflect blame]]
- [[Name the affected reader and the real actor in high-stakes text]]
- [[Distinguish kind softening from deceptive indirectness]]
- [[Doubt claims that new ideas require difficult prose]]
- [[Reject the claim that difficulty improves thinking]]
- [[Use clarity honestly rather than abandoning it]]
- [[Notice when grammar argues for you]]

## Summing up
"Good" writing is ambiguous (ethically sound vs. pragmatically successful). Resolve it: we are ethical writers when we willingly put ourselves in our readers' place and experience what they do. That burdens us to imagine readers. Clear writers are rare and rewarded; crafting a good sentence is its own ethical satisfaction. Whitehead: style is the ultimate morality of mind.

## Quotes
> "Make our ideas no simpler than they deserve, but no more difficult than they have to be."
> "Write to others as you would have others write to you."
