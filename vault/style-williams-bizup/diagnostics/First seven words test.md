# First seven words test

**Purpose:** A near-mechanical test that sidesteps your too-good understanding of your own writing. Writers read into their text what they want readers to get out of it; clarity is "in the eye of more or less informed beholders" (p. 39).

## Procedure
1. For each sentence, underline the first 7-8 words (ignore a short 4-5 word introductory phrase).
2. Ask: is there a character as subject? is there a specific action as verb?
3. If not, mark the sentence a revision candidate and apply [[Diagnose characters and actions]].

## Signal of a problem
- Abstract noun subject; verb not reached within 7-8 words; subject with long modifiers.

## Quick Tips
- When revising a long piece, start with passages that were hard to write because you did not fully understand your ideas; we write badly when unsure.
- Rate clarity with another reader (1-10) and use this analysis to explain differences (Exercise 3.9).

## Second use: sentence shape (L10, Exercise 10.3)
The same underline also checks **shape**: if the subject *and* verb of the main clause do not both appear in the first 7–8 words, the sentence opens with a long introductory element, a long subject, or an interruption.
- Long opener → move it to the end or make it its own sentence ([[Get to the subject of the main clause quickly]])
- Long subject (>7–8 words, often a nominalization or relative clause) → [[Shorten long abstract subjects]]
- Interruption between subject and verb → [[Do not interrupt subject and verb]]
