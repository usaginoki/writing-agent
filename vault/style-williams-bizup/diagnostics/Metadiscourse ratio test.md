# Metadiscourse ratio test

**Purpose:** Detect sentences where talk about the text buries the content.

## Procedure
1. Highlight the words that address the subject matter (in the book's example: *men-women relationships ... greatest changes ... how they work together*).
2. Count: content words versus total. (9 of 34 = problem.)
3. Delete the rest; rebuild with content as subject.
4. For a claim sentence: delete the metadiscourse and rewrite what remains as a sentence. If no claim remains, or the claim is self-evident, you need more thought.
5. Have a reader highlight all metadiscourse in several pages; judge each as useful or redundant, and look for places where it is missing (Exercise 9.5).

## Signal
- Most of the sentence is attribution, announcement, or topic flagging.

Fixes: [[Cut metadiscourse that attributes ideas to a source]], [[Cut metadiscourse that announces your topic]], [[Work topic phrases into the subject]], [[Don't announce your point; state it]]. See [[Metadiscourse]].
