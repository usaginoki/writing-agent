# Diagnose whether an introduction motivates

**Purpose:** check that readers will be motivated and oriented by the introduction.

## Procedure
1. Practical or conceptual? Do you want readers to *do* or to *think*?
2. Draw a line after the introduction. Can't locate the end quickly? Readers can't either.
3. Divide it: shared context + problem + solution/main point. Can't divide quickly? It will seem unfocused.
4. Is the first word after the shared context *but/however* (or other challenge marker)?
5. Split the problem into condition | cost.
   - 5a. Right condition? Practical: exacts a palpable cost. Conceptual: something not known, stated as "we do not know why..." not "What causes...?".
   - 5b. Cost answers "So what?"? Practical: palpable unhappy consequence. Conceptual: a more significant unknown.
6. Underline the solution. It should end the introduction in the stress position and state the key themes (see [[Where's the point test]]).

## Signals of a problem
Any step you can't do quickly; missing cost; point buried or absent; no contrast marker; condition/cost mismatch in type.

## Fixes
[[Pose a problem, not just a topic]] · [[Open with a shared context you can qualify]] · [[Signal the problem with but or however]] · [[State a problem as condition plus cost]] · [[Choose practical or conceptual framing deliberately]] · [[End the introduction with a solution that names key themes]]
Also Exercise 7.3: have a reader run steps 1-6 on your text. See [[L07 Motivation]].
