# Sprawl

**Definition:** Shapeless length: a sentence whose parts trail on as tacked-on subordinate (esp. relative) clauses, or that stops and starts through interruptions. Length alone is not sprawl: a 36-word sentence can be shapely (p. 144).

**Recognize it:** Readers wait too long for the main verb; after the verb they slog through tacked-on clauses (*which… which… that… that…*); interruptions stop and start the sentence. Reading aloud causes hesitation or breathlessness.

**Why it matters:** Long sentences are sometimes necessary; sprawl makes them unclear. Fix by cutting, splitting, converting to modifying phrases, or coordinating.

**Example:** "…genetic engineering, which is a new way…, which are the genes and chromosomes that tell our cells… that constitute our bodies."
