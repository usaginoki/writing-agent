# Folklore Rules

**Definition.** Invented "rules" (mostly from the 18th-20th centuries) that careful, even conservative, writers routinely violate and most readers never notice. The book sorts all rules into: **Real** (define English: articles before nouns; verb-subject agreement), **Social** (Standard vs. nonstandard: *doesn't any* vs. *don't no*), **Invented**. Invented splits into **Folklore** (ignore), **Elegant Options** (formality choices), and **Hobgoblins** (zealously attacked yet harmless; mostly ignore).

**Recognize it.** A prohibition quoted by an editor ("never start with And", "that not which", "less vs. fewer", "since only for time") that best writers contradict. Test: do competent writers regularly violate it and readers not notice? Then it has no force.

**Why it matters for the agent.** Enforcing folklore wastes effort, adds stiffness, and distracts from true imprecision (density, wordiness). Do not "correct" it in others' text; do not obsess in your own.

**Boundary.** Not every item is free: *irregardless* and some word confusions should still be avoided because some readers judge them harshly.
