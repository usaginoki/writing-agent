# Nominalization

**Definition:** A noun derived from a verb or adjective (`discover -> discovery`, `careless -> carelessness`), or a gerund (`our singing`), or a noun identical to the verb (`request, hope, result, repair`). The act of nominalizing turns an action into a thing.
**Recognize:** Suffixes `-tion, -ment, -ence/-ance, -ity, -ness`; frequently preceded by `the` or followed by `of`.
**Why it matters:** Many nominalizations, especially as subjects of verbs, characterize turgid prose; they displace characters from subjects and replace verbs with noun-heavy phrases. Summary: a nominalization *replaces* a verb with a noun, often *displacing* characters from subjects (p. 65).
**Helpful uses:** [[Keep useful nominalizations]].
**Example:** `Our request is that you do a review of the data` -> `We request that you review the data`.
