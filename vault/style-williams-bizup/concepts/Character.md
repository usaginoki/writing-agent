# Character

**Definition:** The person, group, institution or (familiar) abstraction that a sentence is about and who/which performs the actions. Ideally the subject of the verb.
**Recognize:** Ask "Who is doing what?" Characters hide in possessives, `by/of` objects and adjectives (`governmental`), or are absent.
**Kinds:** flesh-and-blood characters (preferred); virtual characters (familiar abstractions such as `studies`, `abortion on demand`) when they are subjects of a series of verbs.
**Why it matters:** Readers want characters as subjects *even more* than actions as verbs (p. 47).
**Example:** Federalists, popular democracy, factions in the Federalist sentence.
