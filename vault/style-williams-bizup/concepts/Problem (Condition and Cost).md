# Problem (Condition and Cost)

**Definition:** a problem has two parts that readers must see *together*: a **condition** (situation with potential for trouble: terrorism, rising tuition, binge drinking) and a **cost** (intolerable consequence readers don't want to pay). No cost, no problem.

**Two kinds:**
| | Practical | Conceptual |
|---|---|---|
| About | a condition in the world | what we think/understand |
| Condition | anything with palpable costs | something not known or understood (a question) |
| Cost | pain, loss, unhappiness | dissatisfaction at not knowing something more important (a larger question) |
| Solution | an action | information / changed understanding |
| Typical writers | outside academia | inside academia |

**Find the cost:** state the condition, imagine "So what?", answer it.

**Example:** Condition: bingeing spreading. Cost: deaths, injuries, destruction of property. Conceptual: condition "we don't know why bingers ignore known risks"; cost "we can't understand risk-taking in general".
