# Style as Choice

**Definition.** Style is the set of choices a writer makes among grammatical versions of the same content. Good writers choose; the book's Part One thesis is that even "correctness" has more choice than rules suggest, and the choices that matter most are those that make prose dense or clear.

**Recognize it.** Two grammatical options exist (e.g., *to slightly conceal* / *to conceal slightly*; *who* / *whom*; *they* / *he or she*) and the difference is tone, clarity, or audience, not truth.

**Why it matters.** Choices define who we are as writers (formal and fastidious vs. plain). The agent must pick on grounds of reader effect, not on memorized prohibitions. Obeying all optional rules always makes you seem formal; ignoring real rules marks you as unschooled.

**Example.** Choose (2) over (1): *We lost the election because the media did not support us.*
