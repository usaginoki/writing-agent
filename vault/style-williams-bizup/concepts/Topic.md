# Topic

**Definition:** what a sentence is "about": the idea the rest of the sentence comments on. Readers take the clearest topic to be a short noun phrase early in the sentence, usually the grammatical subject (p. 72-73).

**Recognize it:** often the subject, but not always. The subject can be a placeholder or "doer" while the topic sits elsewhere: *It is impossible for **your claim** to be proved*; *In regard to **this question**, I believe...*; *It is likely that **our proposal** will be accepted*; *Such results no one could have predicted* (fronted object).

**Why it matters:** a run of topics tells readers what a passage is globally about ([[Topic String]]). Topics should be short, concrete, familiar, and usually main characters ([[Character]]). Old info belongs there ([[Old-New Information]]).

**Point of view:** changing which character is topic shifts perspective (Allies defeated Germany / Germany was defeated by the Allies) (p. 78, Ex. 5.3).

**Note:** the "doer" definition of subject fails; the "about" definition is only partly right. Use subjects to name topics in most (not all) sentences ([[Make subjects name topics]]).
