# Motivation

**Definition:** the reasons readers have to read carefully. Readers attend best to a *problem* important to them, not merely an interesting *topic*. Motivation is created in the introduction, which gets one shot at answering readers' "So what?".

**Recognize it:** an introduction motivates when a reader finishes it knowing what is wrong/unknown, what it costs them, and what is proposed. Without it you have only a topic.

**Why it matters:** engaged, knowledgeable readers fill gaps themselves; most readers are not. Writers must (1) make readers want to read carefully and (2) tell them what to expect (see [[Point]], [[Issue]]).

**Example:** "College students now binge drink..." (topic only) vs. the version adding deaths, injuries, destruction (problem).
