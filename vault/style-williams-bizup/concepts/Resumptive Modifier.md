# Resumptive Modifier

**Definition:** A modifier that repeats a key word (noun, adjective, or verb) from the clause just ended and continues from it, extending the line of thought without a tacked-on relative clause.

**Recognize it:** Comma after a word, the word repeated, then more: *…in this sentence, a sentence that I could have ended at that comma…*. Adjective/verb resumption just repeats the word (*true… and lyrical, true to…, lyrical in…*; *resist… , resist its obsession…*). Noun type often uses *that* or *one that*.

**How to build:** find the noun the clause modifies, pause with a comma, repeat the noun, add a restrictive *that* clause.

**Why it matters:** Lets a clause reach closure, then begin afresh; mature writers use it to extend a line of thought.

**Example:** "I now address a problem we have wholly ignored, one that has plagued societies that sell their natural resources…"
