# Elegance

**Definition:** The grace added to clear prose by balanced syntax, sound and meaning and by artfully stressed endings. It rests on clarity: simple characters as subjects and actions as verbs, plus balance, plus emphasis (p. 185).

**Recognize:** Echoing phrase pairs, a heavy final noun or *of* phrase, rare chiasmus, a long suspended build-up.

**Why it matters:** Bald clarity can be dry; a flash of elegance fixes a thought and gives pleasure on recall. But the book cannot teach how to have it; it can only name devices. Reading elegant writers is how it is absorbed.

**Caution:** Use at key moments only. "Less is more"; compression is the first grace. The test for elegant versus inflated is whether it survives reduction to a clear core.

**Example:** Lippmann's *where that balance no longer exists, democracy perishes ... freedom cannot be maintained*.
