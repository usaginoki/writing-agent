# Subject (Simple and Whole)

**Definition:** The *whole subject* is the full noun phrase before the verb (`The Federalists' argument in regard to the destabilization of government by popular democracy`); the *simple subject* is its head word (`argument`).
**Recognize:** Ask "who or what" before the verb; the simple subject is the noun that agrees with the verb.
**Why it matters:** A character must be the simple subject, not merely inside the whole subject (`the president's veto` vs `the president vetoed`). Long whole subjects before weak verbs signal density. Grade-school lore that subjects are doers and verbs actions is only sometimes true (`The problem was the topic of our discussion`).
**Example:** whole subject `the Wolf's jump out from behind a tree`; simple subject `jump`.
