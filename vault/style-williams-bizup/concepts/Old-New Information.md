# Old-New Information

**Definition:** Old information is what readers already have: words they just read, or general knowledge of the subject (e.g. *astronomers* in a passage about space). New information is what they cannot anticipate: unfamiliar, complex, or technical content.

**Recognize it:** hard to judge in your own prose because to you everything is old. Test with a reader who underlines new information ([[Old-new test]]).

**Why it matters:** readers want familiar-before-unfamiliar and simple-before-complex. Old goes in the [[Topic]] slot (start); new goes in the [[Stress]] slot (end). Main characters, once mentioned, count as old, so [[Character]]-as-subject and old-before-new usually cooperate.

**Example:** "A black hole is created by the collapse of a dead star..." (old *black holes* first; new *collapse* last).
