# Ethics of Style

**Definition:** The view that stylistic choices (subjects, verbs, agents, complexity) are moral as well as aesthetic, because they shape what readers understand, whom they blame, and whether they are manipulated.

**Core rule:** Write to others as you would have them write to you. Ethical writers would willingly trade places with their readers (p. 204).

**Key distinctions**
- Unintended obscurity (innocent; fix, and readers should respond candidly) vs. intended misdirection (breach).
- Necessary complexity (legitimate technical or new-idea content) vs. rationalized opacity (usually wrong).
- Kind softening vs. deception; motive decides.
- "Good" as ethically sound vs. pragmatically successful; the rule resolves the dilemma.
- Clarity is an ideological, constructed value, not a conspiracy; abuse of it is the problem.

**For the agent:** In revising, preserve meaning, responsibility and risk; never use passives, nominalizations, or hedges to remove an agent or soften a danger. The source gives no separate rule on hedging; [[Hedge]] is honest when it reflects real uncertainty, and the ethical test is the reader-swap.
