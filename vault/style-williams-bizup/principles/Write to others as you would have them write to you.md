# Write to others as you would have them write to you

**Rule:** The First Rule of Ethical Writing: you write ethically when you would willingly trade places with your intended readers and experience what they experience after reading your text.
**Why (reader effect):** Readers who find carelessness, laziness or self-indulgence stop reading and stop trusting. Few violate the rule deliberately; most assume their own prose is clear and blame shallow reading.

## Diagnose
- Run the [[Reader-swap test]].
- Ask what the reader will do or believe after reading; would you accept that result if you were the reader?
- Be suspicious of your own clarity judgments: you underestimate readers' needs.

## Revise
1. Imagine the real reader and the consequences of misunderstanding.
2. Cut gratuitous complexity; keep necessary complexity.
3. Where the text serves your interest at the reader's expense, rewrite so the reader's interest is visible.

## Examples
> Test case: a rate-increase notice that never names who raises rates or who pays. Would its writer want to receive it? No, so it breaks the rule.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Writers may be coerced by employers; that does not remove the consequences, though it may mitigate blame (p. 193).
- Does not demand maximal simplicity: see [[Make ideas no simpler than they deserve and no harder than necessary]].
