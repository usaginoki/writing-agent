# Use correlative conjunctions before parallel parts

**Rule:** To emphasize a coordination use *both X and Y, not only X but Y, (n)either X (n)or Y*; place each member before the same part of speech. (p. 159)
**Why (reader effect):** Misplaced members (verb after *not only*, subject after *but*) break the parallel and jar readers.

## Diagnose
- Find *not only/both/either*; check that the following *but/and/or* precedes a word coordinate with the one after the first.

## Revise
1. Move the first member so it precedes the same part of speech as the second.
2. Drop the verb or subject from one side as needed.

## Examples
> ✓ Great Britain is both a good trading partner and a reliable ally in the war against terrorism.
> ✗ …you not only help readers… but you enhance your own image…
> ✓ …you not only help readers understand a complex sentence more easily, but enhance your own image as a good writer.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Optional device; plain *and* is fine when emphasis is not needed.
