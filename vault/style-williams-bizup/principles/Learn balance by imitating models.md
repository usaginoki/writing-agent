# Learn balance by imitating models

**Rule:** Develop a knack for balance by imitating the pattern (not the words) of admired passages, with a subject of your own; read elegant writers until their rhythm becomes habit.
**Why (reader effect):** Balance patterns "don't just shape your thinking; they generate it", pushing you to find the matching second half.

## Diagnose
- Does the writer rarely produce a balanced closer even when the content is parallel?

## Revise
1. Pick a model (sermon, speech, quotation).
2. Copy its outline with a new subject.
3. Finish half-sentences with balancing halves (Exercise 11.1).

## Examples
> Model: Survival in the wilderness requires the energy and wit to overcome... but rewards the person who acquires that power with the satisfaction... and with the confidence...
> Imitation: Life as a college student offers a few years of intellectual excitement but imposes a sense of anxiety on those who look ahead...

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Not word-for-word imitation; not a substitute for clarity.
