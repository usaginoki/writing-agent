# Make important actions verbs

**Rule:** Express each key action of the story in a verb, not in an abstract noun (nominalization) or adjective; avoid vague verbs (`was taking place`, `occurred`, `is`, `has`) that name no action.
**Why (reader effect):** Readers judge a sentence "clear" when they find actions in verbs. Actions in nouns make sentences wordy, indirect and vague, and hide who does what.

## Diagnose
- Box the key actions of the sentence (what happens, what is argued, decided, changed).
- Are they verbs? If they are nouns ending in `-tion, -ment, -ence, -ance, -ity, -ness`, or gerunds, or hidden in adjectives (`applicable`, `indicative`, `argumentative`), they are misplaced.
- Is the main verb a light one (`be, seem, have, make, take, conduct, occur`)?

## Revise
1. Convert each nominalized action to a verb ([[Turn nominalizations into verbs]]).
2. Make the matching character the subject.
3. Replace an empty verb with the new verb, or drop it.
4. Connect clauses with conjunctions.

## Examples
> ✗ Our lack of data prevented evaluation of UN actions in targeting funds to areas most in need of assistance.
> ✓ Because we lacked data, we could not evaluate whether the UN had targeted funds to areas that most needed assistance.

> ✗ ...the Wolf's jump... occurred, causing her fright.
> ✓ ...the Wolf jumped out... and frightened her.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Some nominalizations are useful; see [[Keep useful nominalizations]].
- Stylistic limit: short, all-verb Dick-and-Jane sentences are not the goal ([[Be as complex as necessary and no more]]).
