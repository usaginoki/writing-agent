# Do not hide the responsible agent to deflect blame

**Rule:** When you know who did, will do or is doing something that matters to the reader, say so in the subject. Do not use intransitive verbs, agentless passives, or abstract subjects to remove the responsible party.
**Why (reader effect):** Subjects and verbs imply who is responsible. Hiding the agent steers readers away from asking "By whom?" and is deception when intended.

## Diagnose
- For each sentence with a company, institution or the writer as potential agent, ask: who is the doer? Is that party the subject?
- Look for verbs like *occur*, *be made*, *be authorized*, and abstract subjects (*the restructuring of rates*).
- Run the [[Agency-hiding audit]].

## Revise
1. Put the real agent first and make the action a verb.
2. Name who is affected (the reader) as object or subject.
3. If you intend to hide the agent, reconsider: would you want this written to you?

## Examples
> ✗ ...mistakes may have occurred. (Sears ad; agent vanishes)
> ✓ ...we made mistakes.
> ✗ The new rates move revenues from every class of customer closer to the cost actually incurred to provide gas service.
> ✓ ...we can now make you pay more for your gas service after November 12.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Passives are fine when the agent is unknown or irrelevant; the issue is intent to deceive.
- Emphasizing good intentions with Sears as subject was "self-interested but innocent"; degrees of seriousness vary.
