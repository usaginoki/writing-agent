# Link revised clauses with precise connectors

**Rule:** After turning nominalizations into verbs, join the new character-action clauses with specific conjunctions rather than fuzzy prepositions (`of, by, in, on the part of`). Choose the connective that states the logical relation.
**Why (reader effect):** Prepositions leave relations between actions vague; conjunctions make cause, condition and concession explicit, so the logic is clearer.

## Diagnose
- Chains of `of/in/by` phrases between abstract nouns.
- A sentence with several character-action pairs but no stated relation between them.

## Revise
1. Decide the relation: simple cause (`because, since, when`); conditional (`if, provided that, so long as`); contradicting expected cause (`though, although, unless`).
2. Try templates: *X because Y; Since X, Y; If X, then Y; Although X, Y; X and/but/so Y* (Quick Tip, p. 38).
3. Pick the clause order that tells the real sequence of events.

## Examples
> ✗ Our more effective presentation of our study resulted in our success, despite an earlier start by others.
> ✓ Although others started earlier, we succeeded because we presented our study more effectively.

> ✗ Our loss in sales was a result of their expansion of outlets.
> ✓ We lost sales because they expanded outlets.
