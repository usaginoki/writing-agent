# Write conclusions by reversing the introduction

**Rule:** When nothing better comes to mind, build the conclusion from the introduction's parts in reverse: (1) restate the point; (2) say its significance by answering "So what?" anew or restating the benefit; (3) suggest a further unknown ("Now what?"); (4) optionally echo the prelude with a coda.
**Why (reader effect):** the conclusion is the last thing read; it should gather the point, its significance, and implications for thinking further.

## Diagnose
- Does the conclusion restate the main claim?
- Does it say why that matters (or recast the cost as a benefit)?
- Does it point to a further question?

## Revise
1. State the gist of the main claim.
2. Significance: "If we do not control bingeing soon, many more students will die" or "If we start now, we will save many lives."
3. Further problem: "even if we can control bingeing, risk-taking in general remains."
4. Coda in popular writing (anecdote/quotation/fact echoing prelude).

## Examples
> ✓ "Though we can come at the problem of bingeing from several directions, the most important is education... But that means each university must devote time and resources to it."

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Conclusions vary more than introductions; "there are other ways to conclude." Codas are rare in natural/social sciences.
