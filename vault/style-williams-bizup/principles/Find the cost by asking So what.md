# Find the cost by asking So what

**Rule:** After stating the condition, imagine a reader asking "So what?" and answer it; the answer is the cost. Test the cost the same way.
**Why (reader effect):** it forces you to name the consequence that makes readers care.

## Diagnose
- Read the condition aloud and ask "So what?" Does the next sentence answer it?
- Practical: does the answer state a palpable consequence that causes unhappiness?
- Conceptual: does it state a more significant matter not known or understood?

## Revise
1. Write the answer to "So what?" in 1-3 sentences.
2. If readers might see different or no costs (a publicist sees bad press; a callous reader sees none), describe costs so they matter to *those* readers.
3. If you cannot answer, the problem may not be worth stating; rethink.

## Examples
> condition: "...Bingers drink quickly... to pass out." **So what?**
> ✓ cost: "Bingeing is far from harmless... deaths, many injuries... kills and injures not just drinkers but those around them."
