# End sentences with new information

**Rule:** Put what readers cannot anticipate (new, complex, unfamiliar, technical) toward the end of the sentence.
**Why (reader effect):** readers prefer to read the new and complex after the familiar and simple; the end is also the [[Stress]] position, so new material gets noticed, and complexity is easier to absorb once momentum has built.

## Diagnose
- Underline the last 3-4 words. New and important? If they are old or trivial, the sentence is misordered.
- Look for sentences where a long, technical, or new subject precedes familiar material.

## Revise
1. Move new/complex material after the verb.
2. Let familiar material take the subject slot ([[Begin sentences with old information]]).
3. Use passive, *there*, *what*, *it* shifts as needed ([[Patterns - Moving material to the end]]).

## Examples
> ✗ Questions about the ethics of withdrawing intravenous feeding are more difficult.
> ✓ More difficult are questions about the ethics of withdrawing intravenous feeding.

> ✗ A determination of involvement of lipid-linked saccharides ... in vivo was the principal aim of this study.
> ✓ The principal aim of this study was to determine how lipid-linked saccharides are involved in the assembly of oligosaccharide chains of ovalbumin in vivo.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Don't make a sentence end on a heavy phrase that should be trimmed; trim first ([[Trim the end of the sentence]]).
- Emphasis may override: when you want a different word stressed, see [[Put the words you want emphasized in stress position]].
