# Treat elegant options as tone choices

**Rule:** Split infinitives, *who* for *whom*, a final preposition, and plural *none/any* are all acceptable. Observing the opposite "rule" makes prose slightly more formal; choose it deliberately by audience, never as an imposed fix.
**Why (reader effect):** Few readers notice a violation, but some notice observance as self-conscious formality. Observing all of them all the time earns little credit and sounds stiff.

## Diagnose
- Formal register needed (close scrutiny, conservative audience)? Observe.
- Otherwise leave the natural form; fix only if the split or final preposition is weak/awkward (e.g., a preposition ending a sentence on a weak word).

## Revise
1. Decide formality first.
2. Apply the formal variant (*to conceal slightly*; *a stick with which to beat*; *None ... is*; *whom* when needed) only if wanted.
3. If a sentence ends weakly on a preposition, rework it for stress.

## Examples
> ✓ they wanted to slightly conceal the fact ...  /  ✓ ... to conceal slightly the fact
> ✓ ... a stick to beat the official with.  /  ✓ ... a stick with which to beat the official.
> ✓ None of the reasons are sufficient ...  /  ✓ None of the reasons is sufficient ...
> ✓ The man I met with was the man I had written to.  /  ✓ The man with whom I met was the man to whom I had written. (more formal)

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Orwell's ending *... must never be departed from* is weaker than *We do not defend English just to create a "standard English" whose rules we must always obey*: end on a strong word ([[Stress]]).
