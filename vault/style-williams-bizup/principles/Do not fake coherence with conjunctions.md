# Do not fake coherence with conjunctions

**Rule:** Use *therefore, however, thus, hence, indeed, also, moreover, another* only where a real logical relation exists; no more than a few times a page.
**Why (reader effect):** bluffed connectors don't create connection; readers don't need them when sentences are cohesive and the passage coherent.

## Diagnose
- Circle connectors; for each, can you state the logical relation (contrast, consequence, conclusion)? Struggling writers bluff most; have a reader call your bluffs ([[Faked connection test]]).
- Count *and/also/moreover/another* ("here's one more thing").

## Revise
1. Keep *but/however* for contradiction or qualification; *therefore/consequently* to wind up reasoning.
2. Delete the rest; repair topic strings and old-new order instead.

## Examples
> ✗ ...influences his popularity. Therefore, it should report on the president objectively. Both reporters and the president are human, however, subject to error. Also, people act differently in public... Hence, to understand a person...

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Experienced writers use connectors, but depend on the logical flow of ideas.
