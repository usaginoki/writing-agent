# Order parts chronologically, coordinately, or logically and signal it

**Rule:** Let readers see the principle behind the order of sentences, paragraphs, and sections, and signal it. Chronological: earlier to later, narrative, cause and effect (*first, then, finally; as a result, because of that*). Coordinate: parallel sections like pillars of a roof (*three reasons...*), ordered by importance, complexity etc. (*first, second; also, another, more important, in addition*). Logical: example/generalization, premise/conclusion, assertion/contradiction (*for example, on the other hand, it follows that*).
**Why (reader effect):** without a perceivable order, readers can't judge parts as coherent.

## Diagnose
- Name the ordering principle of each section. Can you?
- Are signal words present?
- Index-card test ([[Index-card order test]]).

## Revise
1. Choose the order that best serves readers.
2. Order coordinate items sensibly (importance, complexity).
3. Add signal words/phrases.

## Examples
> ✓ chronological: "In an essay written before instruction... after four weeks... in three more essays... in a final essay six months after..."

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Chronology is fine when the content is truly a sequence; the problem is defaulting to it ([[Do not narrate your thinking]]).
