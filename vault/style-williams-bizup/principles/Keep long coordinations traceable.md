# Keep long coordinations traceable

**Rule:** When a coordination is so long that readers lose track of where the second element attaches or of pronoun references, shorten the first half, repeat a reminder word, or repeat a noun in place of an ambiguous pronoun. (pp. 162-163)
**Why (reader effect):** Readers feel "a flicker of hesitation about where to connect" (*…and that they do not understand that one day they…*).

## Diagnose
- Second coordinate begins far from where the coordination began; repeated *that* / *they* with unclear antecedents.

## Revise
1. Shorten the first half so the second starts closer.
2. Repeat a word that marks where the coordination began (creates a [[Resumptive Modifier]]).
3. Repeat the noun where a pronoun is ambiguous.

## Examples
> ✗ Teachers should remember that students are vulnerable and uncertain about those everyday ego-bruising moments that adults ignore and that they do not understand that one day they will become…
> ✓ …students are vulnerable to ego-bruising moments that adults ignore and that they do not understand that one day…
> ✓ …vulnerable to ego-bruising moments that adults ignore, to remember that they do not understand… (repeated word)
> ✓ Teachers should remember that students are vulnerable to… and that students do not understand… (repeated noun)

## When not to apply / exceptions
- None stated in source.
