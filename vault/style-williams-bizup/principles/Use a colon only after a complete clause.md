# Use a colon only after a complete clause

**Rule (convention):** Put a colon only after a whole subject-verb-object structure, never in the middle of a clause (between verb and its object list).
**Why (reader effect):** A colon breaking a clause into two fragments splits subject/verb/object connections readers need.

## Diagnose
- Look at the words before each colon: could they stand as a sentence? If not, remove the colon.

## Revise
1. Delete the colon, or
2. Complete the clause ("the following:") and keep the colon.

## Examples
> ✗ Genetic counseling requires: a knowledge of statistical genetics, an awareness of choices...
> ✓ Genetic counseling requires the following: a knowledge of statistical genetics, ...

## When not to apply / exceptions
- A colon/dash after a long list subject, before a summative word, is fine: "...Supreme Court justices: all take an oath..." ([[Never put a single comma between subject and verb]]; [[Summative Modifier]]).
