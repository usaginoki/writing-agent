# Replace a phrase with a word

**Rule:** Where a phrase can be replaced by one precise word, replace it; often this also turns a [[Nominalization]] back into a verb.
**Why (reader effect):** Shorter, more direct prose; actions return to verbs.

## Diagnose
- Look for stock phrases ("the reason for", "despite the fact that", "in the event that", "prior to", "decrease in the number of").
- Look for long descriptive phrases that one word names (*carefully read what you have written* = *edit*).
- No rule says when; the book says only that you often can, so be alert and try (p. 129). It demands vocabulary and wit.

## Revise
1. Find the phrase's core meaning; name a single word for it (see [[Patterns - Phrases to words]]).
2. Check any nominalization in the phrase becomes a verb.
3. Reread for grammar after the swap.

## Examples
> ✗ As you carefully read what you have written ... the thing to do before anything else is to see whether you could use sequences of subjects and verbs instead of the same ideas expressed in nouns.
> ✓ As you edit, first replace nominalizations with clauses.
> ✗ Despite the fact that the data were checked, errors occurred. / ✓ Even though...
> ✗ A sail-powered craft that has turned on its side or completely over must remain buoyant enough so that it will bear the weight of those individuals who were aboard.
> ✓ A capsized sailboat must support those on it.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- None stated in source beyond that no principle predicts the right word; supply it yourself.
