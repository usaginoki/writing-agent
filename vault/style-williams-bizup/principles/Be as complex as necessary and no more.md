# Be as complex as necessary and no more

**Rule:** Make a style as complex as the idea demands, no more. Do not mistake clarity for Dick-and-Jane sentences; do not mistake professional-voice density for rigor.
**Why (reader effect):** A difficult style can needlessly complicate complex ideas as easily as simple ones; dense professional style excludes outsiders and erodes trust. Overly short sentences cannot express mature ideas (see Lessons 10-11).

## Diagnose
- Reading: does the complexity express complex content? Find characters and actions and unravel the passage yourself.
- Writing: can you justify each nominalization and passive? (Exercise 4.7) If not, revise.
- Compare with field norms (Exercise 4.8) but test them against your audience.

## Revise
1. Retell the passage as character-action pairs.
2. Rewrite with characters as subjects and actions as verbs; maybe compress further (Parsons example: three versions, each shorter).
3. Accept some loss of nuance when readers gain access, if trade-off is acceptable.
4. Apply the Writer's Golden Rule: write to others as you would have others write to you.

## Examples
> ✗ Apart from theoretical conceptualization there would appear to be no method of selecting among the indefinite number of varying kinds of factual observation...
> ✓ Without a theory, scientists have no way to select from everything they could say about a subject only that which they can fit into a coherent whole...
> ✓ (more concise) Whatever you describe, you need a theory to fit its parts into a whole.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Some research cannot be made clear to lay readers, but less often than researchers think.
- Revised versions lose nuance and some content; judge the trade-off.
