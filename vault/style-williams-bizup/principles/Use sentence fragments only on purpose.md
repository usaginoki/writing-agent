# Use sentence fragments only on purpose

**Rule:** An accidental fragment (esp. a detached *because...* or *which...* clause) is a serious error to most readers. A deliberate one is a stylistic choice: short (under ~10 words), mimicking a mind at work, an afterthought, often ironic.
**Why (reader effect):** Careless fragments read as uneducated; deliberate ones sound conversational. Academic prose rarely uses them.

## Diagnose
- Find each punctuated sentence lacking a main clause. Starts with *Because, Which, That, Although*?
- If deliberate: short? Will the audience see that you know what you're doing?

## Revise
1. Re-attach the fragment to its main clause with a comma.
2. Or keep it and shorten it to an obvious afterthought.

## Examples
> ✗ ...merely by replacing commas with periods. Because if you do, you will be considered... careless.
> ✓ Twain: "Which I did. And profitably."

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Academic and formal writing: avoid. Test with your audience first.
