# Introduce sections clearly so imperfect paragraphs survive

**Rule:** Not every paragraph needs a perfect point-first structure. Substantial paragraphs should still open with a sentence or two that sets up key terms, but if sections and subsections are introduced clearly, readers forgive paragraphs with the point at the end.
**Why (reader effect):** readers who know the section's point can work through a few imperfect paragraphs; if they don't know what paragraphs add up to, they feel lost no matter how good each is.

## Diagnose
- Does the paragraph's first sentence or two introduce its key terms?
- Is the section's point already stated?

## Revise
1. First fix section/subsection introductions.
2. Then give long paragraphs an opening frame; move the point to the opening segment's end when cheap.

## Examples
> ✗ (2a) opens with fossil sequences; the point ("By mapping every fossil... recreate how and when animals evolved") comes last.
> ✓ (2b) opens with that point sentence, then details.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Short transition and aside paragraphs need no framing.
