# Use the passive to put old information first

**Rule:** Choose a passive verb when it lets the old, familiar element become the subject and puts the new element at the end. Ignore "avoid passives" when context calls for one.
**Why (reader effect):** the passive exists in the language to arrange old and new information in the right order; it creates flow and puts stress where it belongs.

## Diagnose
- Is the active sentence's subject new to readers while its object (or agent) was just mentioned? Then flip.
- Check the previous sentence's ending vs. this subject.

## Revise
1. Make the old element (often the object) the subject; verb becomes passive.
2. Put the former subject (the new info) in a *by*-phrase at the end, or drop it if irrelevant.
3. Check that the new ending sets up the next sentence.

## Examples
> ✗ ...studying black holes in space. The collapse of a dead star into a point perhaps no larger than a marble creates a black hole.
> ✓ ...studying black holes in space. A black hole is created by the collapse of a dead star into a point perhaps no larger than a marble.

> ✗ Some claim that our genes influence aspects of behavior that we think are learned. Our genes, for example, seem to determine...
> ✓ Some claim that aspects of behavior that we think are learned are in fact influenced by our genes. Our genes, for example, seem to determine...

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Default remains active when old/new order doesn't require flipping; see [[Passive and Active]] for when the passive is a problem.
