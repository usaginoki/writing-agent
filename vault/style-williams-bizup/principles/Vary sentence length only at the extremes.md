# Vary sentence length only at the extremes

**Rule:** Do not plan sentence length unless nearly all your sentences are shorter than about fifteen words or longer than about thirty. If the occasion allows, use length on purpose: short for urgency, terse certainty or directness; very long, controlled sentences for sinuous or immersive effect.
**Why (reader effect):** Uniformly short or long sentences tire readers; deliberate length can mimic action or mind. Ordinary editing for clarity varies length naturally.

## Diagnose
- Count words across a paragraph: all under ~15? all over ~30?
- Ask whether any short/long sentence is doing a job (urgency, certainty, mimicry).

## Revise
1. Edit for clarity and concision first; length varies naturally.
2. If monotonous, combine or split on purpose.
3. For deliberate length, keep control with coordination and free/resumptive modifiers (Mailer).
4. Short sentences: Trotsky (urgency), Pound (certainty), Twain (mimicked directness, with long sentences winking at knowing readers).

## Examples
> The attackers decided to break down the fence. They broke down part of it and set fire to the rest. (Trotsky, urgency)
> No teacher has ever failed from ignorance. (Pound, terse certainty)

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Mailer-style sentences are products of "premeditated art", not overflow; do not mimic casually.
