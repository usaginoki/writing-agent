# Delete obvious implications

**Rule:** Cut what readers can infer from the rest of the sentence, even when the redundancy is diffuse rather than word-for-word.
**Why (reader effect):** Readers already supply the implied content; stating it spends their attention for nothing.

## Diagnose
- Ask of each clause: would a reader reach this from what else I said? (*learn* implies *trying*; *rules* implies *playing the game*; *chess* is a *game*.)
- Hardest redundancy to spot because it spreads across several words (p. 129).

## Revise
1. List what each key word implies.
2. Delete the words that restate those implications.
3. Reread for sense.

## Examples
> ✗ Imagine someone trying to learn the rules for playing the game of chess.
> ✓ Imagine learning the rules of chess.
> ✗ Energy used to power industries and homes will in years to come cost more money.
> ✓ Energy will eventually cost more.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- None stated in source. Keep an implication that your audience (e.g. novices) cannot be assumed to share.
