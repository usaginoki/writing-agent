# Announce key themes in the stress of the opening sentence

**Rule:** Put key words naming the organizing concepts at the end of the first (framing) sentence of a passage.
**Why (reader effect):** readers look to the last few words of the opening sentence(s) for the concepts a paragraph will develop; if the organizing idea doesn't appear until midway, the passage feels unfocused.

## Diagnose
- [[Opening sentence test]]: underline the sentence that frames the paragraph; circle its important words; do they sound like a title for the paragraph? Are they at its end?

## Revise
1. List the 2-3 key concepts the passage develops.
2. Rewrite the opening so they appear at its end.
3. Repeat them through the passage ([[Thread themes through a passage]]).

## Examples
> ✗ Great strides in the early and accurate diagnosis of Alzheimer's disease have been made in recent years. (the real point, the risk of informing, appears late)
> ✓ In recent years, researchers have made great strides in the early and accurate diagnosis of Alzheimer's disease, but those diagnoses have raised a new problem about informing those most at risk who show no symptoms of it.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Applies to fairly long paragraphs (not 2-3 sentence introductory/transitional ones) and to passages of any length, even a whole document.
