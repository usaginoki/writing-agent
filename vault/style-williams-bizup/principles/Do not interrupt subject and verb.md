# Do not interrupt subject and verb

**Rule:** Do not insert a long element between a subject and its verb. Move it to the front or the end of the sentence, whichever connects it to what precedes or follows. (p. 147)
**Why (reader effect):** Readers "hold their mental breath" until the verb arrives.

## Diagnose
- Subject, comma, clause/phrase, comma, verb: the verb is delayed.

## Revise
1. Decide whether the interrupter ties more closely to the previous or the next sentence.
2. Move it to the beginning or the end, per step 1; the next sentence then picks up on the last-stated element ("This lack of communication…" or "It is a kind of style…").
3. Short interruptions (one adverb) are tolerable.

## Examples
> ✗ Some scientists, because they write in a style that is impersonal and objective, do not easily communicate with laypeople.
> ✓ Since some scientists write in a style that is impersonal and objective, they do not easily communicate with laypeople. This lack of communication damages…
> ✓ Some scientists do not easily communicate with laypeople because they write in a style that is impersonal and objective. It is a kind of style filled with passives…
> ✓ Some scientists deliberately write in a style that is impersonal and objective. (short interruption OK)

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Short interruptions are minded less (p. 147).
