# Balance uncoordinated parts of a sentence

**Rule:** Balance can also link parts that are not grammatically coordinate: subject against object, a relative-clause predicate against the main predicate, direct object against object of a preposition, subordinate against main clause.
**Why (reader effect):** Readers hear the echo in sound and sense even without a conjunction, so the sentence feels architectural rather than linear.

## Diagnose
- Look for a long sentence whose beginning and end are about opposites or mirror ideas but share no shape.
- Check whether subject-side and object-side material can mirror (scientists / those of us; research creates views / construe reality).

## Revise
1. Identify two parts that deserve to be set against each other.
2. Shape them alike (same length, same kind of phrase) and pair word meanings (short / minor / small).
3. Put the heavier or longer member second.

## Examples
> Scientists whose research creates revolutionary views of the universe invariably confuse those of us who construe reality from our common-sense experience of it.
> Those of us concerned with our school systems will not sacrifice the intellectual growth of our innocent children to the social engineering of incompetent bureaucrats.
> Were I trading scholarly principles for financial security, I would not be writing short books on minor subjects for small audiences.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Same caution as all elegance devices: use at key moments only.
