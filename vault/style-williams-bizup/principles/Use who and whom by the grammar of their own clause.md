# Use who and whom by the grammar of their own clause

**Rule:** *Who* is the subject of a verb in its own clause; *whom* is an object in its own clause. Always *whom* after a preposition. Conversational *who* for *whom* in a question is an elegant-option matter; in relative clauses use the test below.
**Why (reader effect):** Formal readers notice *whom* misuse; plain *who* at a question's start rarely bothers anyone.

## Diagnose
- Use [[Who-whom deletion test]].

## Revise
1. Relative clause: delete pronoun; if still sensible, *whom*; if not, *who*.
2. Pronoun begins a clause that is the object of a verb: use the clause's grammar.
3. After a preposition: *whom*.

## Examples
> ✓ The committee chose someone whom they trusted. (deletable)
> ✓ The committee chose someone who earned their trust. (not deletable)
> ✓ The committee chose someone in whom they had confidence.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- *Who am I writing for?* (question) is fine in nonformal prose; *For whom am I writing?* is formal.
