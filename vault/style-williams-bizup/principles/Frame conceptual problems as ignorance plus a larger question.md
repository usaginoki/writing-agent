# Frame conceptual problems as ignorance plus a larger question

**Rule:** State the condition as something not known or understood (as a statement, not a bare question); state the cost as a more important, larger question the answer would help resolve. Your small question inherits its importance from the larger one.
**Why (reader effect):** readers care about the large question; without the link the small question looks pointless.

## Diagnose
- Is the condition a statement of what we don't know ("we do not know why bingers ignore known risks") rather than "What causes bingeing?"
- After "So what?", does the cost name a bigger unknown?
- Can you imagine the small answer helping the larger question? If not, the question may not be worth asking.

## Revise
1. Write: "X does not know A. If they knew, they might understand B (larger, more important)."
2. Pick a question small enough to answer with your resources (time, funding, skill, pages) but linked to a large one.

## Examples
> ✗ "What color were Lincoln's socks at Gettysburg?" (no larger question)
> ✓ "How did Lincoln plan the Address?" (leads to his creative process)

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Limited resources may block addressing a large question directly; that is why you answer a small one linked to it.
