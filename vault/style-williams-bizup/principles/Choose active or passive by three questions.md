# Choose active or passive by three questions

**Rule:** "Write in the active voice" is decent advice with exceptions. Choose voice by asking: (1) Must readers know who did it? (2) Which voice moves readers more smoothly from sentence to sentence (old before new)? (3) Which gives a consistent, appropriate point of view?
**Why (reader effect):** Passive adds words and may delete the agent, but it can also give a short familiar subject, or keep a consistent viewpoint; blanket bans produce worse sentences.

## Diagnose
- Grammatical test: passive = form of `be` + past participle; subject = goal; agent in `by` or dropped.
- Don't confuse with *feel*: `Problem management requires cost control` is grammatically active but feels passive (actions are nominalizations, abstract subject, no characters).
- Run the three questions on each passive/active in doubt.

## Revise
1. Q1 -> [[Use the passive when the agent is unknown or irrelevant]].
2. Q2 -> [[Use the passive to put familiar information first]].
3. Q3 -> [[Hold one point of view across sentences]].
4. If none favors the passive, prefer the active with a character subject.
See [[Patterns - Active and passive choice]].

## Examples
> ✗ Problem management requires cost control. (active but feels passive)
> ✓ We can manage the problem if we control costs.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Writers sometimes use passive to hide a responsible agent (`the flaw was uncorrected`): see Lesson 12 on deliberate impersonality ([[Ethics of Style]]).
- Metadiscourse verbs prefer active/first person ([[Use I and we with metadiscourse verbs]]).
