# Extend sentences with resumptive, summative, and free modifiers

**Rule:** To lengthen a sentence without sprawl, close a grammatically complete segment with a comma, then add a modifying phrase that repeats a key word (resumptive), sums up the clause (summative), or comments on the subject of the nearest verb (free). Do not tack on relative clauses willy-nilly. (pp. 153-155)
**Why (reader effect):** Each modifier lets the sentence reach closure, then begin afresh, so the line extends while readers keep its shape.

## Diagnose
- A trailing *, which…* (or *which…, which…*) attached to a noun or to the whole clause.

## Revise
1. [[Resumptive Modifier]]: find the noun the clause modifies, comma, repeat the noun, continue with restrictive *that* clause. Can also repeat an adjective or verb (no relative clause), or use *one that*.
2. [[Summative Modifier]]: after a complete clause, comma, add a noun phrase summing it up (*a demographic event*), then *that*-clause.
3. [[Free Modifier]]: after a complete clause, comma, add an *-ing* (or past-participle, or adjective) phrase about the subject; it can also open the sentence.

## Examples
> ✗ …what I have not done in this sentence, which I could have ended at that comma…
> ✓ …what I am about to do in this sentence, a sentence that I could have ended at that comma but extended to show you how resumptive modifiers work.
> ✗ Economic changes have reduced Russian population growth to less than zero, which will have serious social implications.
> ✓ …to less than zero, a demographic event that will have serious social implications.
