# Handle generic pronouns without bias

**Rule:** Do not gratuitously offend: avoid generic *he*. Choose the least-bad workaround for the context and accept that every option costs something. Singular *they* is increasingly acceptable.
**Why (reader effect):** *He* alienates; *he or she/s/he* is clumsy; plural *they* can be ambiguous; alternating *she/he* irritates some; abstraction loses the person.

## Diagnose
- Singular indefinite or common noun (*someone, everyone, a writer*) followed by a pronoun.
- Check whether a plural rewrite creates ambiguous referents.

## Revise
1. Try plural subject, but check *they/their/them* references stay clear.
2. Try first person *we* if ambiguity is absent.
3. Try singular noun repetition or a single *he or she* occasionally; avoid *s/he*, *he/she*.
4. Consider singular *they* where the audience tolerates it.
5. Impersonal abstraction only if it does not become vague.

## Examples
> ✗ If a writer ignores the ethnicity of his or her readers, s/he may respond ...
> ✓ If we ignore the ethnicity of our readers, they may respond in ways we would not expect to words that to us are innocent of bias.
> ✓ No one should turn in their writing unedited.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Formal usage still requires singular agreement (*Everyone ... he*) but that conflicts with bias avoidance; the book predicts eventual acceptance of singular *they*. See [[Patterns - Generic pronoun options]].
