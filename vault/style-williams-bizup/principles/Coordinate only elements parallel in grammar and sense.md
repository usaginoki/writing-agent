# Coordinate only elements parallel in grammar and sense

**Rule:** Coordinate elements of the same grammatical form (clause with clause, prepositional phrase with prepositional phrase) and that are coordinate in thought. Do not join unrelated claims with bare *and*. (pp. 160-162)
**Why (reader effect):** Mismatched forms read as offensive lack of [[Parallelism]]; strings of *and* hide the logical relation between claims.

## Diagnose
- Underline each coordinate element; compare grammatical forms (gerund phrase + *that* clause?).
- Each *and*: what is the real relation (cause, contrast, addition)? ([[Coordination]])

## Revise
1. Grammar: recast one element to match (both *that*-clauses, or both gerunds).
2. Sense: replace *and* with *because, but, so*, etc.; supply the relationship.
3. No rule detects non-coordinate thought: "Watch for it."

## Examples
> ✗ The committee recommends revising the curriculum… and that the division be reorganized…
> ✓ …recommends that the curriculum be revised… and that the division be reorganized… / …recommends revising the curriculum… and reorganizing the division…
> ✗ Grade inflation is a problem at many universities, and it leads to a devaluation of good grades… and will not be solved simply by grading harder.
> ✓ Grade inflation is a problem at many universities because it devalues good grades that were earned by hard work, but it will not be solved simply by grading harder.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Careful writers accept some nonparallel coordination: a noun phrase with a *how* clause (*the problems of education… and how coordinated efforts can address them*), an adverb with a prepositional phrase (*quickly, carefully, and with the help of many*). (p. 162)
