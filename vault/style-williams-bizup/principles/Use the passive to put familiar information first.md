# Use the passive to put familiar information first

**Rule:** Use a passive when it lets you begin with short, familiar information and end with long, complex, new information; avoid sentences that open with new, complex subjects.
**Why (reader effect):** Readers rely on the sentence opening for context; opening with new complex material confuses. The familiar-to-new order is easier.

## Diagnose
- Is the subject long, new, and complex while the object repeats something from the prior sentence?
- Underline whole subject; compare with what was just said.

## Revise
1. Swap: make the familiar element (`our decision`) the subject via passive.
2. Move the heavy bundle after the verb (`by the weight we give...`).
3. Check the next sentence's start ([[Old-New Information]]).

## Examples
> ✗ ...The weight given to industrial competitiveness as opposed to the value we attach to the liberal arts will determine our decision.
> ✓ ...Our decision will be determined by the weight we give to industrial competitiveness as opposed to the value we attach to the liberal arts.

> ✗ Research demonstrating the soundness of our reasoning and the need for action supported this decision.
> ✓ This decision was supported by research demonstrating the soundness of our reasoning and the need for action.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- If the active version already opens with familiar information, keep it.
