# Name the affected reader and the real actor in high-stakes text

**Rule:** In warnings, notices and legal-compliance messages, make the reader (you) and the responsible party (we) subjects of the key verbs, and keep dangerous actions as verbs, not nominalizations or passives.
**Why (reader effect):** A recall letter built on parts as subjects (a defect, this plate, its failure, hood fly-up) and passive/nominalized dangers lulls the reader. A GAO study found half of recall letters failed to prompt action. A text can meet a legal duty and still evade an ethical one.

## Diagnose
- List subjects of every sentence. Are they objects and conditions rather than people?
- Mark nominalizations and passives that describe harm (*failure*, *be misaligned*, *hood fly-up*, *warning*).
- Is the reader mentioned only as a possessive (*your vehicle*)?

## Revise
1. Make *you* the subject of consequences: If you brake hard and the plate fails, you could crash.
2. Make *we* the subject of what the company knows and will do.
3. Turn nominalized dangers into verbs; state the severe outcome plainly.

## Examples
> ✗ A defect... may exist on your vehicle... failure could affect vehicle directional control...
> ✓ If you brake hard and the plate fails, you could lose control and crash.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- The exercise asks which wording is "closer to the truth": *you could crash* vs. *your car could crash*; both are choices, so pick for the reader's risk.
