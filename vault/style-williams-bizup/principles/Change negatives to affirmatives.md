# Change negatives to affirmatives

**Rule:** Express an idea affirmatively when you can (*not careful* to *careless*; *not many* to *few*).
**Why (reader effect):** A negative costs an extra word and forces readers to do "algebraic calculation" (*not different* = *same*).

## Diagnose
- Underline *not, no, never, none* and negative prefixes in a sentence.
- Underline implicit negatives: verbs (*preclude, prevent, lack, fail, doubt, reject, avoid, deny, refuse, exclude, contradict, prohibit, bar*), prepositions (*without, against, lacking, but for, except*), conjunctions (*unless, except when*).
- Two or more negatives together = high risk (see [[Untangle stacked negatives]]).

## Revise
1. Find the affirmative word for the negated idea (see [[Patterns - Negative to affirmative]]).
2. Rewrite the clause around it.
3. Check the meaning is unchanged.

## Examples
> ✗ Do not write in the negative. / ✓ Write in the affirmative.
> ✗ There is no reason not to believe that engineering malfunctions in nuclear energy systems cannot be anticipated.
> ✓ Malfunctions in nuclear energy systems will surprise us.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Keep the negative when you want to emphasize the negative (the book jokes it could have written "Keep a negative sentence when...").
