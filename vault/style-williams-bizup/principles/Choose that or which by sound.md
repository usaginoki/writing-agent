# Choose that or which by sound

**Rule:** Restrictive *which* is not an error. The "that for restrictive, which for nonrestrictive" rule dates from Fowler (1906); even Fowler admitted most best writers don't follow it. Real usage: nonrestrictive clauses take comma + *which*. For restrictive, either works; prefer *that* for a softer sound, and *which* when *that* repeats nearby.
**Why (reader effect):** Sound and rhythm; readers do not notice the restrictive *which*.

## Diagnose
- Nonrestrictive test: can the noun be identified without the clause? Then comma + *which*.
- Restrictive clause with *which*: leave unless two *that*s crowd.

## Revise
1. Nonrestrictive: ensure comma + *which* (never *that*).
2. Restrictive: default *that*; switch to *which* if two *that*s sit within a word or two.

## Examples
> ✓ ABCO Inc. ended its first bankruptcy, which it had filed in 1997.
> ✓ ABCO Inc. sold a product that made millions.
> ✓ We all have that one rule which we will not give up.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- If a house style mandates that/which, follow it as style, not grammar.
