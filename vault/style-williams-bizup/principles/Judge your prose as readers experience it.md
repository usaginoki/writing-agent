# Judge your prose as readers experience it

**Rule:** Do not trust your own sense that a draft is clear. Evaluate what the words on the page do to a reader, not what you meant.
**Why (reader effect):** Writers read their intended thoughts into their text, so it always seems clearer to them than to readers; they then send it off when it satisfies themselves, not the reader.

## Diagnose
- Ask: would someone without my knowledge of the topic get this on a first read?
- Watch for sentences that feel clear only because you know what is behind them.
- Note why a sentence feels dense/abstract vs. clear/direct (the later lessons name the features).

## Revise
1. Read the page, not your head; read as a stranger.
2. Where a reader would stumble, rewrite until the reader's need, not yours, is met.

## Examples
> ✗ Recognition of the fact that systems of grammar differ from one language to another can serve as the basis for serious consideration of the problems confronting translators ...
> ✓ When we recognize that languages have different grammars, we can consider the problems of those who translate great works of literature into English.
