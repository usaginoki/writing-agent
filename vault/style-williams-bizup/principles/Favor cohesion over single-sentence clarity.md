# Favor cohesion over single-sentence clarity

**Rule:** When clarity principles conflict, prefer old-before-new over characters-as-subjects and actions-as-verbs.
**Why (reader effect):** how you order old and new determines how cohesive readers find the text; a passage's cohesion trumps the clarity of individual sentences.

## Diagnose
- A sentence is perfectly "clear" (character subject, action verb) but follows poorly from the previous one.

## Revise
1. Decide order by old/new first; then fix subject/verb within that constraint.
2. Often no conflict: characters already mentioned are old.

## Examples
> ✗ (active, character-ish, but new first) The collapse of a dead star ... creates a black hole.
> ✓ (passive, old first) A black hole is created by the collapse of a dead star...

## When not to apply / exceptions
- The three principles usually complement each other; the priority matters only when they clash.
