# Convert although and since clauses into independent clauses

**Rule:** A long introductory *although* or *since* clause is easy to turn into an independent clause: drop *although* and start the next clause with *but/however*; drop *since* and start the next with *so/therefore/as a result*. (pp. 155-156)
**Why (reader effect):** Removes the long opener that delays the main subject and verb.

## Diagnose
- Long sentence opening with *Although…* or *Since…*.

## Revise
1. Delete the subordinator.
2. Join with a comma plus the connector (*but, however, so, therefore, as a result*) in front of the old main clause.

## Examples
> ✗ Although some writers write well on their own, without the help of direct teaching or models of good prose, most benefit from instruction…
> ✓ Some writers write well on their own, without the help of direct teaching or models of good prose, but most benefit from instruction…
> ✗ Since few writers write well on their own… most first-year college students would benefit from a course in composition.
> ✓ Few writers write well on their own… so most first-year college students would benefit from a course in composition.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- None stated in source. (Not every opener converts easily; other clause types: move or split, see [[Get to the subject of the main clause quickly]].)
