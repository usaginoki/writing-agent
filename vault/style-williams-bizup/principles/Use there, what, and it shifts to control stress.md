# Use there, what, and it shifts to control stress

**Rule:** To move a subject or clause into stress, use a *there is/are* shift, a *what ... is* shift, or an *it ... that* shift.
**Why (reader effect):** each shifts material right where readers expect new information and emphasis.

## Diagnose
- A key unit of new info is stuck in the subject slot, or a long noun clause precedes a short predicate.

## Revise
1. *There* shift: *Several syntactic devices let you...* becomes *There are several syntactic devices that let you...* Don't ban all *there is*. Starting a paragraph with *there* introduces new topics developed afterward.
2. *What* shift: *We need a monetary policy that...* becomes *What we need is a monetary policy that...*
3. *It* shift: *That oil prices would be set by OPEC once seemed inevitable* becomes *It once seemed inevitable that oil prices would be set by OPEC.*

## Examples
> ✗ We need a monetary policy that would end fluctuations in money supply, unemployment, and inflation.
> ✓ What we need is a monetary policy that would end fluctuations in money supply, unemployment, and inflation.

> ✗ That oil prices would be set by OPEC once seemed inevitable.
> ✓ It once seemed inevitable that oil prices would be set by OPEC.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Some editors discourage all *there is/are*; the book says they are useful for shifting.
