# Set off long interruptions with paired punctuation

**Rule:** Avoid interrupting subject-verb or verb-object except for emphasis; if the interruption is more than a few words, use paired commas, dashes or parentheses (never semicolons, never a single mark). Place adverbial phrases at front, middle (paired commas) or end according to the emphasis wanted.
**Why (reader effect):** Interruptions make the grammar harder to follow; pairing shows where they start and end.

## Diagnose
- Look for material between subject/verb or verb/object.
- One comma on only one side? Missing partner.

## Revise
1. Move the interruption to the front or end if possible.
2. Otherwise pair the marks.
3. For a tacked-on clause at the end: comma only if non-essential.

## Examples
> ✓ A sentence, if it includes subordinate clauses, may seem to need commas.
> ✓ In recent years modern poetry has... / Modern poetry has, in recent years, become... / ...in recent years. (different stresses)

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Short interrupters may need no marks.
