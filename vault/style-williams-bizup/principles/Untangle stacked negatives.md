# Untangle stacked negatives

**Rule:** When a sentence combines *not* with implicit negatives (*except, fail, without, deny, unless*), and perhaps passives and nominalizations, rewrite it completely in affirmative terms, stating the real condition and the action.
**Why (reader effect):** Readers must resolve each negation in turn; stacked negatives baffle completely.

## Diagnose
- Count explicit plus implicit negatives; two or more is a flag.
- Check for passives and nominalizations riding with them (the worst case).

## Revise
1. Work out what the sentence actually requires or permits.
2. State it in the form "if you do X, do Y" or "to get Z, do X" with real people as subjects.
3. Turn nominalizations to verbs and passives to active (see [[Nominalization]], [[Passive and Active]]).

## Examples
> ✗ Except when you have failed to submit applications without documentation, benefits will not be denied.
> ✓ You will receive benefits only if you submit your documents. / ✓ To receive benefits, submit your documents.
> ✗ There should be no submission of payments without notification of this office, unless the payment does not exceed $100.
> ✓ If you pay more than $100, notify this office first.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- None stated in source. (Exercise 9.2: "unless you don't cancel" vs "to avoid being charged, cancel before the trial ends"; the second is clearer.)
