# Open with a shared context you can qualify

**Rule:** Begin with a shared context: history, a recent event, a common belief, or a literature review: something readers know or readily accept, which you then qualify or reject.
**Why (reader effect):** readers accept an apparently unproblematic base, then the *but* delivers a small surprise ("you think you know the whole story, but you don't") that motivates reading on.

## Diagnose
- Does the first sentence(s) state something readers already know or accept?
- Does the problem challenge that context, or is the context unrelated/just a topic dump?

## Revise
1. Choose a form: historical background, recent event, belief, or (in journals) a literature review that you will correct.
2. Keep it brief (a sentence or two; paragraphs only for literature reviews).
3. Follow with *but/however* + the problem ([[Signal the problem with but or however]]).

## Examples
> ✓ Event: "A recent State U survey showed 80% of first-years engaged in underage drinking... a fact that should surprise no one. But what is worrisome is the spread of a new kind of drinking..."
> ✓ Belief: "Most students believe college is a safe place to drink... And for the most part they are right. But for those caught up in 'binge' drinking..."

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Not every piece opens this way; some jump straight to the problem.
