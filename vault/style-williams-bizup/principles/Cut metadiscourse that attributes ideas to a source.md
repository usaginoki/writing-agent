# Cut metadiscourse that attributes ideas to a source

**Rule:** Don't announce that something has been observed, noticed, noted, determined; state the fact.
**Why (reader effect):** Attribution phrases bury the claim and add passives.

## Diagnose
- Look for *it has been observed/noted/determined that*, *have been shown to*, *have been determined to have*.
- Test: does the sentence still say the same thing without the attribution?

## Revise
1. Delete the attribution verb phrase.
2. State the fact directly with its real subject.
3. Keep a citation or named source if the reader needs the source (see exceptions).

## Examples
> ✗ High divorce rates have been observed to occur in areas that have been determined to have low population density.
> ✓ High divorce rates occur in areas with low population density.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- The book says "usually" cut; attribution is legitimate where credit or evidence status matters (cf. the hedging role in [[Calibrate hedges]]). Metadiscourse is needed in moderation.
