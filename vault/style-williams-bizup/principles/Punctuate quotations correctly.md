# Punctuate quotations correctly

**Rule (conventions, American):** Period or comma goes inside the closing quotation mark. Question mark, colon or semicolon go outside, unless the quoted material itself is a question or exclamation (then it stays inside and your own period/comma is dropped). With nested quotes, comma or period goes inside both marks. Quoted final punctuation is replaced by your sentence's needed mark.
**Why (reader effect):** Small errors lead readers to doubt your care with big matters (p. 237).

## Diagnose
- Check every closing quotation mark: what mark sits next to it and who owns it (quote or your sentence)?

## Revise
1. Move period/comma inside.
2. Move ?, :, ; outside unless part of the quote.
3. Drop your own comma after a quoted ? or !.

## Examples
> ✓ President Nixon said, "I am not a crook."
> ✓ My first bit of advice is "Quit complaining"; my second is "Get moving."
> ✓ Freud famously asked, "What do women want?"
> ✓ She said, "I have no idea how to interpret 'Ode to a Nightingale.'"

## When not to apply / exceptions
- None stated in source.
