# End a sentence with a colon, dash or parenthesis for effect

**Rule (stylistic choice):** To join two grammatical sentences with extra effect: colon = formal, "that is/for example/therefore", or balance; dash = casual afterthought; parentheses = short aside inside another sentence (single period outside).
**Why (reader effect):** Marks a writer's sophistication and control of rhythm; a colon signals balance of structure, sound and meaning.

## Diagnose
- Does the second clause explain, illustrate or answer the first? Colon.
- Is it a quick afterthought? Dash or parentheses.
- Overuse of any mark becomes a tic.

## Revise
1. Replace the period/semicolon with the chosen mark.
2. After a colon followed by a full sentence, capitalize only if you want more emphasis (handbooks vary).
3. With parentheses, no period inside; one outside.

## Examples
> ✓ Dance is not widely supported: no company operates at a profit...
> ✓ Civil disobedience is the public conscience of a democracy: mass enthusiasm is the public consensus of a tyranny.
> ✓ Stonehenge is a wonder—only a genius could have conceived it. / ... a wonder (only a genius could have conceived it).

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Do not use a colon to split a clause into two non-sentences: see [[Use a colon only after a complete clause]].
