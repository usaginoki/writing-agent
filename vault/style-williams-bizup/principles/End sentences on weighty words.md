# End sentences on weighty words

**Rule:** End a sentence on words that deserve stress: nouns (heaviest: nominalizations) over adjectives/adverbs over prepositions. Best: a coordinate pair of heavy nouns. Do not end on a preposition or other light word.
**Why (reader effect):** Readers expect stress near the end; a light ending feels anticlimactic. How a sentence begins determines clarity; how it ends determines rhythm and grace.

## Diagnose
- Read the last 3-4 words of each important sentence aloud. Are they light (to, with, of-less tail, adverb)?
- Underline the final word: preposition, adverb, pronoun = weak.
- Nominalizations are bad in subjects but good at the end (p. 174).

## Revise
1. Restructure so the preposition is not last (move the verb with its preposition earlier or rephrase).
2. Move the heaviest, newest information to the end.
3. Consider nominalizing the final action and pairing two nouns: *the rescue and the liberation*.

## Examples
> ✗ ...projects that only the most politically naive psychologist would be willing to give support to.
> ✓ ...projects that only the most politically naive psychologist would be willing to support.
> ✗ ...until the New World rescues us.
> ✓ ...until the New World, with all its power and might, steps forth to the rescue and the liberation of the old. (Churchill)

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Banal plain wording is fine when plainness is the goal; the devices are for sentences that matter.
- Avoid nominalizations in subjects; the endorsement is for ends only.
