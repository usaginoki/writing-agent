# Never put a single comma between subject and verb

**Rule (hard rule):** Do not put a single comma between a subject and its verb, or between a verb and its object, however long the subject.
**Why (reader effect):** The comma cuts the basic grammatical connection readers need.

## Diagnose
- Find the whole subject and its verb ([[Grammar Terms]]); is a lone comma between them? Same for verb and object.
- Is the subject long or a list?

## Revise
1. Shorten the subject so the urge disappears.
2. For a necessarily long list subject, close it with a colon or dash plus a summative word (*all*), or restructure.
3. Real interruptions need paired marks: [[Set off long interruptions with paired punctuation]].

## Examples
> ✗ A sentence that consists of many complex subordinate clauses ... that all precede a verb, may seem to some students to demand a comma.
> ✓ The president, the vice president, ... and Supreme Court justices: all take an oath...

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Paired commas around an interruption are fine.
