# Use apostrophes only for contractions and possessives

**Rule (Real Rules, few options):** Apostrophe in contractions (*don't, it's*). Never to form plurals, except lower-case single letters and capital *A, I, U* (*i's, t's, A's, I's*). Numbers and multi-capital words take plain *s* (*ABCs, 1950s, 767s*). Possessive: singular noun + *'s* (*FDR's*); singular noun ending in s-sound: apostrophe only (*Sophocles' plays*; some handbooks use *'s*, be consistent); plural in s: apostrophe only (*workers' votes*); compound noun: last word (*sister-in-law's*).
**Why (reader effect):** Violations earn "withering abuse" from those who police them (p. 226).

## Diagnose
- Every apostrophe: contraction, possessive, or one of the two plural exceptions? Otherwise delete.
- *its* (possessive) vs *it's* (it is).

## Revise
1. Remove plural apostrophes (*horse's* as plural is wrong).
2. Fix possessives by noun type.
3. Contractions: check the reader's tolerance; academics often avoid them.

## Examples
> ✗ bus's, fence's (as plurals)
> ✓ the ABCs, CDs, 1950s; Dot your i's and cross your t's.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Contractions can seem too casual in academic prose; ask your instructor.
