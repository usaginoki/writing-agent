# Delete redundant pairs

**Rule:** When two near-synonyms are joined by *and*, keep one.
**Why (reader effect):** Doubling adds length with no added meaning; it sounds padded, not learned.

## Diagnose
- Look for "X and Y" where X and Y mean the same thing (full and complete, each and every).
- Origin: early English writers paired a French/Latin word with a native one to sound learned; today most pairs are just redundant (p. 127-128).

## Revise
1. Pick the plainer or more precise member; delete the other and the *and*.
2. If the whole clause is padded, recompress (see example).

## Examples
> ✗ If and when we can define our final aims and goals, each and every member of our group will be ready and willing to offer aid and assistance.
> ✓ If we define our goals, we will all be ready to help.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- None stated in source. (Legal or ritual doublets are outside the book's scope; check genre.)
