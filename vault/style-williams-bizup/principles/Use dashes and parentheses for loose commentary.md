# Use dashes and parentheses for loose commentary

**Rule (stylistic choice):** Loose commentary can go in paired commas (neutral), dashes (casual, and necessary when the commentary has internal commas), or parentheses (sotto voce aside, dates, translations).
**Why (reader effect):** Dashes/parentheses make the boundaries of a long or comma-laden insertion visible, so readers don't lose the subject.

## Diagnose
- Does the inserted material contain commas (lists)? Commas around it will confuse.
- Is it an aside or a gloss?

## Revise
1. Swap paired commas for paired dashes (lists, emphasis) or parentheses (asides, explanatory footnote inside a sentence).
2. Keep both marks of the pair.

## Examples
> ✗ The nations of Central Europe, Poland, Hungary, ... Serbia have for centuries...
> ✓ The nations of Central Europe—Poland, Hungary, ... Serbia—have for centuries been in the middle of...
> ✓ Lamarck (1744-1829) was a pre-Darwinian evolutionist.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Formal writing: a dash is informal; use commas or a colon.
