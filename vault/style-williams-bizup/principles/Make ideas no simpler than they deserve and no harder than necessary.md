# Make ideas no simpler than they deserve and no harder than necessary

**Rule:** Match difficulty to the ideas, not to the writer's habits. Readers must read hard enough to follow genuinely hard ideas (technical content cannot be turned into Dick-and-Jane prose), but writers must not add difficulty beyond what the content requires.
**Why (reader effect):** Readers work hard until they decide the writer did not; then they leave. Einstein: as simple as possible, but not simpler.

## Diagnose
- Is the difficulty in the idea (a drag-force formula with defined variables) or in the sentence (nominalizations, buried agents)?
- Would the intended expert reader find the technical term necessary? Then keep it.
- Would a clear rewrite lose any real content?

## Revise
1. Keep the necessary technical terms and structure.
2. Strip avoidable complexity: unclear subjects, abstractions, long strings.
3. Define terms the reader needs; do not dumb down meaning.

## Examples
> Technical sentence on drag force on a particle (F = 0.5 C_D u^2 A): an engineer cannot make it clear "to everyone", and need not.
> ✗ A major condition affecting adult reliance on early communicative patterns is the extent to which the communication has been planned prior to its delivery.
> ✓ When we speak spontaneously, we rely on patterns of child language.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- The authors of the dense passage may object that the plain version oversimplifies; check what the reader will remember the next day.
- "Whatever can be written can be written clearly", and with a bit more effort, more clearly still (Wittgenstein plus the book's nuance).
