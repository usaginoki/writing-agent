# Don't announce your point; state it

**Rule:** When stating your solution/claim ([[Point]]), delete the metadiscourse and rewrite what remains as a sentence making a claim, not announcing a topic.
**Why (reader effect):** Metadiscourse lets a writer announce a topic ("I examine...") without advancing a claim, so the problem gets no real solution (ties to [[Problem (Condition and Cost)]]).

## Diagnose
- Find "In this study I examine / this paper discusses / I will argue..." in the point of an introduction.
- Strip it; see if a claim remains.

## Revise
1. Delete the metadiscourse.
2. Rewrite the remainder as a declarative sentence with a subject and verb.
3. If the resulting claim is self-evident, you need to say more or think more about what you want to say.

## Examples
> ✗ In this study, I examine the history of Congressional legislation to protect children in the workplace.
> ✓ Congress has legislated to protect children in the workplace. (Self-evident; add a real claim.)

## When not to apply / exceptions
- A road map is still useful for long documents; the rule targets announcements that stand in for the claim.
