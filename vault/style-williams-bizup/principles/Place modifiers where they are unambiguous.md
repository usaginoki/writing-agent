# Place modifiers where they are unambiguous

**Rule:** Put a modifier where readers cannot attach it to two things; move it next to the word it modifies, or to the start of the sentence. (p. 163)
**Why (reader effect):** Readers are unsure what the modifier modifies (especially one at a clause end that could attach to a near or distant phrase).

## Diagnose
- Ask of each adverbial or end-of-clause modifier: could it modify two things?

## Revise
1. Move the modifier adjacent to its true target, or
2. Move it to the front of the sentence, or between the elements where its reading is unambiguous.

## Examples
> ✗ Overtaxing oneself in physical activity too frequently results in injury. (overtaxing, or injuries, frequent?)
> ✓ Overtaxing oneself too frequently in physical activity results in injury. / …results too frequently in injury.
> ✗ Scientists have learned that their observations are as subjective as those in any other field in recent years.
> ✓ In recent years, scientists have learned that… / Scientists have learned that in recent years their…

## When not to apply / exceptions
- None stated in source.
