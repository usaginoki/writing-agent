# Delete redundant modifiers

**Rule:** Cut a modifier whose meaning is already implied by the word it modifies (*final outcome*, *past history*, *completely revolutionize*).
**Why (reader effect):** Readers process the same idea twice; the prose feels padded.

## Diagnose
- For each adjective/adverb ask: does the noun/verb already contain this meaning?
- Typical: *future* plans, *past* history, *final* outcome, *unexpectedly* surprises, *completely* revolutionize.

## Revise
1. Delete the implied modifier.
2. Sometimes the modified word can also go: *revolutionize society* becomes *revolutionary events*.

## Examples
> ✗ Do not try to predict future events that will completely revolutionize society, because past history shows that the final outcome of minor events unexpectedly surprises us more.
> ✓ Do not try to predict revolutionary events, because history shows that the outcome of minor events surprises us more.
> ✗ In the business world of today, official governmental red tape seriously destroys initiative among individual businesses.
> ✓ Government red tape destroys business initiative.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- None stated in source. Distinguish from useful modifiers (see [[Delete useless adjectives and adverbs]]).
