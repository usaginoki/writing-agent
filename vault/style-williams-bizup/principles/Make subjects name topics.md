# Make subjects name topics

**Rule:** In most (not all) sentences, let the grammatical subject name what the sentence is about, and make sure that topic is familiar in context.
**Why (reader effect):** readers read the early short noun phrase as the topic; when subjects are placeholders (*it*, *I*) or the real topic sits elsewhere, the passage feels unfocused.

## Diagnose
- For each sentence: what is the real topic (what the rest comments on)? Is it the subject?
- Watch *it is ... that*, *In regard to X, I...*, fronted objects.

## Revise
1. Name the topic; make it the subject.
2. Keep it short, concrete; prefer main [[Character]]s.
3. Confirm it is familiar ([[Begin sentences with old information]]).

## Examples
> ✗ It is impossible for your claim to be proved. / It is likely that our proposal will be accepted.
> (topics *your claim*, *our proposal* are buried; revise to e.g. *You cannot prove your claim.* / *Our proposal will likely be accepted.*)

> ✗ Consistent ideas toward the beginnings of sentences help readers understand what a passage is about.
> ✓ Readers understand what a passage is generally about when they see consistent ideas toward the beginnings of sentences.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- "Most, not necessarily all" sentences. Fronted objects etc. can be deliberate (*Such results no one could have predicted*).
