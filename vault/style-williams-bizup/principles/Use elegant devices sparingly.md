# Use elegant devices sparingly

**Rule:** Treat balance, climactic endings, chiasmus and suspension as accents for key moments (a main point, the close of an argument); the default is bald clarity. When in doubt, cut: less is more.
**Why (reader effect):** Used to excess, the devices seem merely clever; their impact depends on rarity. Knowing the devices does not confer elegance; wide reading and practice do.

## Diagnose
- Count flourishes per page; more than one or two means ornamentation.
- Ask of each: does it serve an important point or only display skill? (Johnson: strike out the passage you think particularly fine.)
- Check for "extravagant" constructions combining all devices (Oates, Turner): fine in rare moments, not a house style.

## Revise
1. Keep only devices attached to the most important claim of a section.
2. Strip the rest to plain subject-verb sentences.
3. If the result reads dry, add one balanced closer, not more.

## Examples
> ✗ Four flourishes in one paragraph (balance, chiasmus, suspension, echo)
> ✓ Plain paragraph that ends with one balanced sentence on its point

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Rhetorical genres (speeches, closing lines) may reasonably use more.
- Dryness is a risk of simplicity but "well worth taking" (Maugham).
