# Do not interrupt verb and object

**Rule:** Do not wedge a long phrase or clause between a verb and its object; move it to the start or end of the sentence, depending on what comes next. (p. 147)
**Why (reader effect):** Readers want to get past the verb to its object quickly.

## Diagnose
- Verb, comma, long element, comma, object.

## Revise
1. Trim wordiness in the interrupter and object (*regarding* → *about*).
2. Move the interrupter to the front if the next sentence continues about the main clause's end; to the end otherwise.
3. Exception route: if a movable prepositional phrase is shorter than a long object, put it between verb and object.

## Examples
> ✗ We must develop, if we are to become competitive with other companies in our region, a core of knowledge regarding the state of the art in effective industrial organizations.
> ✓ If we are to compete with other companies in our region, we must develop a core of knowledge about the state of the art in effective industrial organizations. Such organizations provide…
> ✓ We must develop a core of knowledge about … effective industrial organizations if we are to compete with other companies in our region. Increasing competition…
> ✓ In a long sentence, put at its end the newest and most important information that you want your reader to remember. (short phrase before long object)

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Short phrase before a long object may sit between verb and object (p. 148).
