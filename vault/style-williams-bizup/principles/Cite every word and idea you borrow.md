# Cite every word and idea you borrow

**Rule:** Avoid anything that might lead an informed reader to think you take credit for words or ideas not yours. (1) Exact words: cite with page numbers and put in quotation marks or a block quote. (2) Paraphrase: cite with pages; no quotation marks, but fully recast in your own words and sentence structure. (3) Idea or method: cite; omit page numbers if the whole source is about it. Applies to any source: print, online, recorded, oral.
**Why (reader effect):** Plagiarism is theft of words and credit; "we read words, not minds", so even innocent carelessness looks dishonest (p. 231). Credibility, grade and reputation are at stake.

## Diagnose
- Mark every sentence whose words or idea came from a source. Is each cited?
- Any material found online treated as free? Still cite.
- Exact source words without marks?

## Revise
1. Add citation to each borrowed idea/paraphrase/quote.
2. Add quotation marks or block format to every verbatim string.
3. Rewrite too-close paraphrase: [[Paraphrase in a new structure, not with synonyms]].

## Examples
> ✗ (unmarked) copying a source's sentence with citation but no quotation marks
> ✓ Levin claims that we experience the theater as the most social form... (cited paraphrase)

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Ordinary phrases anyone would use need neither quotes nor citation (p. 236).
