# Open sentences with short simple elements

**Rule:** Begin with a short introductory phrase or clause, then a short concrete subject, then a specific-action verb; save length and complexity for after the verb. Carry readers from simplicity to complexity.
**Why (reader effect):** momentum built in the first nine or ten words carries readers through complex grammar and meaning that follow.

## Diagnose
- Count words before the main verb. Long subject (e.g. 22 words) plus technical terms up front = struggle.
- Which of two versions begins simply?

## Revise
1. Shorten the subject; make it concrete/familiar.
2. Move heavy modifiers, clauses, technical terms after the verb ([[Patterns - Moving material to the end]]).
3. If needed, nominalizations become verbs ([[Nominalization]]).

## Examples
> ✗ A sociometric and actuarial analysis of Social Security revenues and disbursements for the last six decades ... is the subject of this study.
> ✓ In this study, we analyze Social Security's revenues and disbursements for the last six decades, using sociometric and actuarial criteria...

> ✗ Lincoln's claim that the Civil War was God's punishment ... appears in the last part of the speech.
> ✓ In the last part of his speech, Lincoln claims that God gave the Civil War to both North and South as a punishment for slavery.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Avoid opening many sentences with long phrases/clauses; occasional ones are fine. Readers want this pattern in subordinate clauses too (p. 95).
