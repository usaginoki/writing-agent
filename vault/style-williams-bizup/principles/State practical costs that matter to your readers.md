# State practical costs that matter to your readers

**Rule:** For a practical problem, state its costs explicitly, even if they seem obvious, in terms your actual readers will feel.
**Why (reader effect):** readers may see different costs or none; if they don't see costs affecting them, they have no reason to care. The goal: readers ask "What do we do?" not "So what?".

## Diagnose
- Is the problem named only by a condition word?
- Would a skeptical reader (publicist, callous reader) see a different or zero cost?

## Revise
1. Spell out palpable costs (death, money, loss).
2. Add costs for the specific audience if theirs differ ("hurts our image with parents").
3. Then offer the action that removes or reduces the costs.

## Examples
> ✗ (condition only) "Bingeing is spreading."
> ✓ "...cited in at least six deaths, many injuries, and considerable destruction of property."

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Any condition can be a problem if its costs make readers unhappy; no condition is intrinsically a problem.
