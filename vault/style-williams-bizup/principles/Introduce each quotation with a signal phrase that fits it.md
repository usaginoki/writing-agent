# Introduce each quotation with a signal phrase that fits it

**Rule:** When you introduce a quotation (*Smith says / As Smith asserts / According to Smith / In Smith's view*), choose the verb for your attitude: *says, asserts, claims, suggests, thinks, wants to believe* differ. Punctuation: comma after *Smith said,* and capitalize as in the original; with *that* (*said that*), no comma and no capital.
**Why (reader effect):** The verb tells readers how far you endorse the source.

## Diagnose
- Is the verb neutral where you mean skeptical (or the reverse)?
- Check comma and capital after the signal phrase.

## Revise
1. Choose the verb to match your stance.
2. Apply the comma/capital rule.

## Examples
> ✓ Williams said, "An acceptable but artless way ... is simply to drop it in."
> ✓ He went on to say that "if you introduce the quotation with ... do not use a comma."

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Weaving into your own grammar removes the need for a stand-alone signal phrase.
