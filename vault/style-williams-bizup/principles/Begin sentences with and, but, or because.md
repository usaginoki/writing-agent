# Begin sentences with and, but, or because

**Rule:** Starting a sentence with *and*, *but*, or *because* is grammatical. Fix only overuse (a style issue) and true fragments. Put a *because* clause at the end when it carries new information; use *since* to open with a causal clause that is familiar.
**Why (reader effect):** Readers expect known information first and new information last, so a fronted *because* clause (usually new) is mildly awkward; *since* signals "you already know this".

## Diagnose
- Is the sentence a fragment (*Because it was incomplete.* standing alone)? Fix by attaching.
- Does a sentence open with *Because ...* that states new information? Consider moving it.
- Many *And/But* openers in a row = style problem.

## Revise
1. Keep legitimate openers.
2. Move new-information *because* clauses to the end.
3. For a fronted familiar cause, switch to *since*.

## Examples
> ✗ Because some writers are seized by the idea that writing is good only when it's free of errors that only a grammarian can explain, they write graceless prose.
> ✓ Some writers write graceless prose because they are seized by the idea that writing is good only when it's free of errors ...
> ✓ Since our language seems to reflect our quality of mind, it is easy for those inclined to look down on others to think that grammatical "errors" indicate mental or moral deficiency.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- The placement advice has exceptions; it is "generally sound". The *because* folklore appears in no handbook.
