# Calibrate intensifiers

**Rule:** Use [[Intensifier]]s sparingly; a claim stated moderately invites thoughtful consideration, an aggressive one invites resistance. Remember the absence of a hedge is itself the most common intensifier.
**Why (reader effect):** Readers reflexively doubt *obviously, undoubtedly, it is clear that* (they think the opposite). Bulldozer prose closes off reasoned response.

## Diagnose
- List intensifying adverbs, adjectives (*key, crucial*), verbs (*prove, show*), and absolutes (*all, every, any, completely, totally, not a person*).
- Notice unhedged claims: tone comes from the absence of hedges.

## Revise
1. Delete *obviously, clearly, undoubtedly, of course* and *it is clear that*.
2. Replace absolutes with quantified or moderate terms (*all* to *many*, *every* to *most*, *totally* to *virtually*).
3. Add a hedge where evidence is not conclusive.
4. Keep an intensifier only where it earns emphasis.

## Examples
> ✗ For a century now, all liberals have argued against any censorship of art, and every court has found their arguments so completely persuasive that not a person any longer remembers... censorship is totally a thing of the past.
> ✓ For about a century now, many liberals have argued against censorship of art, and most courts have found their arguments persuasive enough that few people may remember exactly... censorship is virtually a thing of the past.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Some call the moderate version "wordy and weak" (the book concedes "Perhaps"). Confident writers use intensifiers less often than hedges but they are not banned.
