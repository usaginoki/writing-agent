# Suspend the climax with parallel openers

**Rule:** To build a dramatic climax, you may open a sentence with a series of parallel, coordinated phrases or clauses and delay the point until the end, which should be the longest member.
**Why (reader effect):** Delay creates tension that the final clause discharges. It deliberately reverses the usual advice to put the point first.

## Diagnose
- Is this the sentence that ends an argument or chapter? Does it hold a point worth waiting for?
- Does the opening series share one form (three *if* clauses)?

## Revise
1. Convert the preliminary conditions into parallel *if/when/although* clauses.
2. Close with a triple coordination, longest last, ideally ending on of + nominalization (*worthy of esteem*).

## Examples
> If journalists held themselves as responsible for... ; if they considered... ; if they did all or any of these things, they would make journalism more useful, public life stronger, and themselves far more worthy of esteem. (Fallows)

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Impact is inversely proportional to frequency: "the less it's used, the bigger its bang."
- Do not use for ordinary sentences; those should still the point-first advice of Lesson 10 where concision principles apply (Lesson 10).
