# Give dangling modifiers an explicit subject

**Rule:** A modifier dangles when its implied subject differs from the explicit subject of the main clause. Make the implied subject explicit inside the modifier, or (better) make it the main clause's subject. (pp. 163-164)
**Why (reader effect):** The reader looks for who does the action and finds an unnamed agent.

## Diagnose
- Opening *to + verb* / participial phrase: who does it? Is that the main subject?

## Revise
1. Make the subject explicit in the modifier clause (*If developed countries are to overcome…*), or
2. Rewrite so the main subject is the doer (*…developed countries must commit themselves to…*), usually by turning the abstract subject into an action ([[Agent]]).

## Examples
> ✗ To overcome chronic poverty and lagging economic development in sub-Saharan Africa, a commitment to health and education is necessary for there to be progress…
> ✓ If developed countries are to overcome chronic poverty…, a commitment to health and education is necessary…
> ✓ To overcome chronic poverty… developed countries must commit themselves to…

## When not to apply / exceptions
- None stated in source.
