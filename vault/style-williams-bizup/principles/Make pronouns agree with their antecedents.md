# Make pronouns agree with their antecedents

**Rule:** Verbs agree with subjects; pronouns agree in number with referents. For collective nouns (*committee, faculty, staff*), use singular when the group acts as one, plural when members act individually (plural is the rule in British English; American use varies).
**Why (reader effect):** Real rule: disagreement marks the writer as careless.

## Diagnose
- Find each pronoun; identify its referent; check number.
- Collective noun: does it act as a unit or as individuals?

## Revise
1. Match number (*efforts ... they*).
2. Collective: choose consistently within the sentence.
3. Indefinite/gendered singulars: see [[Handle generic pronouns without bias]].

## Examples
> ✗ Early efforts ... failed because it ignored political issues.
> ✓ Early efforts ... failed because they ignored political issues.
> ✓ The committee has met but has not yet made its decision.
> ✓ The faculty have the memo, but not all of them have read it.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Casual *they* for *everyone/a person* is widely used; formal usage requires singular.
