# Doubt claims that new ideas require difficult prose

**Rule:** Treat "my ideas are too new to be clear" as probably wrong; test it case by case. Readers are owed precise, nuanced prose but do not owe unlimited time to unpack it.
**Why (reader effect):** Truth is not the only value in the marketplace of ideas; the cost of finding it counts. Such claims are "more often wrong than right."

## Diagnose
- Could a competent reader, given reasonable time, extract the claim? Could you restate it in plain sentences?
- Are terms defined; is the complexity in content or syntax?

## Revise
1. Attempt a plain paraphrase of the sentence's claim.
2. Keep what is lost in paraphrase as genuine nuance; restate the rest plainly.
3. Add a bit more effort for still more clarity.

## Examples
> ✗ ...the ruse of desire is calculable for the uses of discipline ... a discourse of splitting that violates the rational, enlightened claims of its enunciatory modality. (Bhabha; is it subtle or babble?)

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Some cases cannot be settled "to everyone's satisfaction"; the book admits this.
- Real technical vocabulary is legitimate; see [[Make ideas no simpler than they deserve and no harder than necessary]].
