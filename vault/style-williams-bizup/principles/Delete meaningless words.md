# Delete meaningless words

**Rule:** Cut verbal tics that carry no meaning: kind of, actually, particular, really, certain, various, virtually, individual, basically, generally, given, practically.
**Why (reader effect):** They are throat-clearing; they dilute the real content and slow reading. (Principle 1 of six, see [[L09 Concision]].)

## Diagnose
- Scan for the tic list (see [[Patterns - Meaningless words]]).
- Delete the word; if the meaning is unchanged, it was filler.

## Revise
1. Delete each tic.
2. If a tic hid a real qualification (e.g. *generally* = "in most cases"), keep it as a deliberate [[Hedge]] instead.
3. Re-read; tighten what remains.

## Examples
> ✗ Productivity actually depends on certain factors that basically involve psychology more than any particular technology.
> ✓ Productivity depends on psychology more than on technology.
> ✗ ...various technical problems, but these did not really affect the election's actual result.
> ✓ ...technical problems, but these did not affect the election's result.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Words that look like tics but do real work (a genuine hedge, "certain" meaning "specific, known") stay. The book gives no mechanical test beyond "does deleting change meaning?".
