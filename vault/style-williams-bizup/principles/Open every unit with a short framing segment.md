# Open every unit with a short framing segment

**Rule:** Begin each unit (sentence, paragraph, section, subsection, whole document) with a short, easily grasped segment that frames the longer, more complex segment following. Sentence: subject/topic or main clause expressing the point. Paragraph: one or two sentences. Section: a paragraph or more. Document: one or several paragraphs, still substantially shorter than the body.
**Why (reader effect):** readers judge as clear any unit that opens this way; they know what to expect and which knowledge to bring.

## Diagnose
- Section by section, draw a line after the short opening segment (Exercise 8.1). Can't find it? It is missing.
- Does it state the [[Point]] and signal themes?
- Repeat paragraph by paragraph.

## Revise
1. Write or isolate a short opening for the unit.
2. End it with the point ([[Put the point sentence at the end of the opening segment]]).
3. Name key themes in it.
4. Check the opening is much shorter than the rest.

## Examples
> ✗ "Resistance in Nevada against its use as a waste disposal site has been heated."
> ✓ "Nevada has heatedly resisted its use as a waste disposal site."
> ✗ (point buried at end) "Greater knowledge of pre-Columbian civilizations... has led to a historical reassessment of Columbus' role."
> ✓ "Historians are reassessing Columbus' role in world history, because they know more about pre-Columbian civilizations..."

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Short transitional or aside paragraphs need no such structure.
- Literary essays exploring your thinking for patient readers may ignore it (see [[Accept formulaic structure for clarity]]).
