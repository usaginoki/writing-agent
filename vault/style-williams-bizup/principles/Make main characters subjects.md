# Make main characters subjects

**Rule:** Make the *simple subject* of most clauses a short, concrete word naming a main character, not a noun that merely has the character attached to it (possessive, `of`/`by` phrase, adjective). Characters *in* the whole subject are not enough; they must *be* the subject (p. 44).
**Why (reader effect):** Readers expect to find "who" in the subject slot and "what they do" in the verb. When a character is buried or deleted, the sentence feels abstract, indirect and dense; readers must reconstruct the story (or guess wrongly). The book calls this the *first* principle of clear style, ranked above putting actions in verbs (p. 47).

## Diagnose
- Underline the first 7-8 words (skip a 4-5 word introduction); see [[Diagnose characters and actions]] / [[First seven words test]].
- Is the simple subject a flesh-and-blood character (person, group, institution) or a short familiar concept? If it is an abstract noun (esp. a nominalization), flag it.
- Is the character present only as a possessive (`the Wolf's jump`), a `by`/`of` object, an adjective (`governmental`), or absent entirely?
- Long whole subjects (many words before the verb) with a weak verb (`was based`, `occurred`) are a signal.

## Revise
1. List the characters (people, groups, organizations first).
2. Find the action each performs (see [[Turn nominalizations into verbs]]).
3. Make the character the subject of the verb for that action.
4. Join the resulting clauses with logical connectors ([[Link revised clauses with precise connectors]]).
5. If no character exists, supply one ([[Name characters in subjects instead of deleting them]]).

## Examples
> ✗ ...as a walk through the woods was taking place on the part of Little Red Riding Hood, the Wolf's jump out from behind a tree occurred...
> ✓ Little Red Riding Hood was walking through the woods, when the Wolf jumped out from behind a tree and frightened her.

> ✗ The Federalists' argument in regard to the destabilization of government by popular democracy was based on their belief...
> ✓ The Federalists argued that popular democracy destabilized government, because they believed...

> ✗ The president's veto of the bill infuriated Congress. (character only *in* subject)
> ✓ When the president vetoed the bill, he infuriated Congress.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Not every subject must be a person: familiar abstractions can serve as virtual characters ([[Make abstractions virtual characters only when familiar]]).
- Passive is right when it yields a better subject for flow, emphasis or agent unknown ([[Choose active or passive by three questions]]).
- "Most" verbs, not all; the aim is match between story level and grammar level, not mechanical personification.
