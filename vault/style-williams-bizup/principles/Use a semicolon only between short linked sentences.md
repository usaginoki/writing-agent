# Use a semicolon only between short linked sentences

**Rule:** A semicolon is a soft period: both sides must be grammatical sentences. Use it when the first is under ~15 words and the second is closely tied to it. Also use it before *however/therefore/thus* starting a new sentence (keep the comma after *however*). *Choice* to use it; *rule* about what goes on each side.
**Why (reader effect):** Readers hear a semicolon as a sentence boundary. It signals tight linkage and sophistication; about once every couple of pages is enough (p. 211).

## Diagnose
- Find every semicolon: is each side a complete grammatical sentence? (Exceptions: series with internal commas, p. 225.)
- Find "..., however," joining two sentences: that is a splice.

## Revise
1. Replace comma + *however/therefore* between sentences with semicolon + word + comma.
2. If either side is long or internally punctuated, use a period.
3. Never end an introductory clause with a semicolon ([[Follow the five rules for commas after introductory elements]]).

## Examples
> ✗ Taxpayers have supported public education, however, they now object because taxes have risen so steeply.
> ✓ Taxpayers have supported public education; however, they now object...

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Semicolon + coordinating conjunction is allowed when both sentences are long with internal commas, though a period is better still (p. 212).
- Semicolons also group series items that contain commas ([[Punctuate a series with commas or semicolons]]).
