# Cut metadiscourse that announces your topic

**Rule:** Don't tell readers what the sentence is "about"; say the thing. Reduce "This section introduces...; The first thing to say about it is..." to a plain statement.
**Why (reader effect):** Readers find the topic more easily when announcements are trimmed; only a few words in the sentence then address the subject.

## Diagnose
- Look for *this section introduces, the first thing to say is, in this study I examine, the last point I would like to make is, it is important to keep in mind*.
- Count words addressing the subject matter versus talking about the text. In the book's example only 9 of 34 words addressed the subject (p. 133-134).

## Revise
1. Underline the content words.
2. Delete the rest of the metadiscourse.
3. Rebuild the sentence with the content as subject; make it still more direct if possible.

## Examples
> ✗ The last point I would like to make is that in regard to men-women relationships, it is important to keep in mind that the greatest changes have occurred in how they work together.
> ✓ The greatest changes in men-women relationships have occurred in how they work together. ✓✓ Men and women have changed their relationships most in how they work together.
> ✗ This section introduces another problem, that of noise pollution. The first thing to say about it is that...
> ✓ Another problem is noise pollution. First, it exists not only...

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Keep guiding words (*first, second, therefore, on the other hand*); readers need some structural metadiscourse in everything (p. 137).
