# Extend sentences with coordination

**Rule:** Use multiple, balanced [[Coordination]] as the foundation of a gracefully shaped long sentence, instead of tacking elements on one by one. It is harder than modifiers but more graceful. (pp. 156-159)
**Why (reader effect):** Coordinate elements give readers a visible structure; a string of tacked-on clauses gives none.

## Diagnose
- Long sentence whose parts are linked by *which/while/that* chains where items could be listed as equals.

## Revise
1. Find the parallel ideas (the minor / unfinished / botched; should / should not be done; good / honest nourishment).
2. Recast them as coordinated words, phrases, or clauses.
3. Order short to long ([[Order coordinate elements from short to long]]) and verify parallel in grammar and sense ([[Coordinate only elements parallel in grammar and sense]]).
4. Optional emphasis: correlative conjunctions ([[Use correlative conjunctions before parallel parts]]).

## Examples
> ✗ The aspiring artist may find that even a minor, unfinished work which was botched may be an instructive model for how things should be done, while for the amateur spectator, such works are the daily fare which may provide good, honest nourishment, which can lead to an appreciation of deeper pleasures that are also more refined.
> ✓ (Hoffman) For the aspiring artist, the minor, the unfinished, or even the botched work, may be an instructive model for how things should—and should not—be done. For the amateur spectator, such works are the daily fare which provide good, honest nourishment—and which can lead to appreciation of more refined, or deeper pleasures.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- None stated in source. Elegant prose elaborates balanced coordination further (Lesson 11).
