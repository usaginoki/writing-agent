# Draft first, then revise with principles

**Rule:** Get a draft down fast; apply style principles afterward to find and fix sentences likely to trouble readers. Do not impose them as rules on each sentence while drafting.
**Why (reader effect):** Drafting under rules stalls writing; revising clarifies the writer's own ideas, which in turn yields clearer prose. Readers get better text, but only within the time available.

## Diagnose
- Is the text a first draft? Then do not polish sentence style yet; first get ideas down.
- In revision: flag sentences that feel dense, abstract, or indirect and test them against the principles.

## Revise
1. Draft quickly.
2. Re-read, mark sentences likely to give readers trouble.
3. Revise those with the relevant principle; repeat as time allows.
4. Stop at "as good as can be made in the time"; do not chase perfection.

## Examples
> ✗ An understanding of the causal factors involved in excessive drinking by students could lead to their more effective treatment.
> ✓ We could more effectively treat students who drink excessively if we understood why they do so.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Prestige occasions (e.g., an inaugural address) may call for more than clarity; see Part Four ([[Elegance]]).
- Principles are aids, not laws (Mencken's warning against reducing style to rules).
