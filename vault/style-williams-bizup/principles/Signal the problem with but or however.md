# Signal the problem with but or however

**Rule:** Make the first word of the first sentence after the shared context *but*, *however*, *on the other hand*, or another word announcing a challenge to that context.
**Why (reader effect):** without an explicit contrast readers can miss the shift from context to problem.

## Diagnose
- Find the sentence where the shared context ends. Is its first word a contrast marker?

## Revise
1. Insert *but/however* at the head of the problem sentence.
2. If it still doesn't read as a challenge, tighten the context so it is something the problem actually qualifies.

## Examples
> ✗ "...college students drink and often drink hard. A new kind of drinking known as 'binge' drinking is spreading..."
> ✓ "...drink and often drink hard. **But** a new kind of drinking known as 'binge' drinking is spreading..."

## When not to apply / exceptions
- None stated in source (introductions without shared context lack the move entirely).
