# Use the passive when the agent is unknown or irrelevant

**Rule:** Use a passive when the doer is unknown, self-evident, or of no interest to readers.
**Why (reader effect):** Naming the obvious or unknowable agent adds clutter; the passive puts the reader's interest (the goal) in the subject.

## Diagnose
- Would an active version force you to invent or state a doer the reader doesn't care about (`someone rumors`)?
- Is the agent self-evident (the voters reelect presidents; police/courts fine; custodians keep records)?

## Revise
1. Move the goal to the subject; use `be` + past participle.
2. Omit the `by` phrase.

## Examples
> ✓ The president was rumored to have considered resigning.
> ✓ Valuable records should always be kept in a safe.
> ✗ The voters reelected the president with 54% of the vote. ✓ The president was reelected with 54% of the vote.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- If readers must know who is responsible, use the active.
- Do not use the passive to hide responsibility deceptively (`the flaw was uncorrected`; Lesson 12).
