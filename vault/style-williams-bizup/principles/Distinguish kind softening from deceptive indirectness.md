# Distinguish kind softening from deceptive indirectness

**Rule:** Indirectness motivated by kindness (*I'm afraid our funding didn't come through* meaning *you have no job*) is not unethical when the reader understands it; indirectness that conceals what the reader needs to know is.
**Why (reader effect):** Readers decode polite euphemism correctly when the context makes meaning plain; they are harmed when the soft phrasing hides risk, cost or responsibility.

## Diagnose
- Will the reader reach the true meaning? Does the softening cost them information they need to act?
- Whose interest does the indirectness serve, theirs or yours?

## Revise
1. Keep softening where it cushions without hiding.
2. Where stakes are high or action is needed, state it plainly.

## Examples
> ✗ ...a defect may exist on your vehicle... could result in vehicle crash. (softened beyond need)
> Acceptable: *I'm afraid our new funding didn't come through.*

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Motive is the test (p. 196): the same grammar may be kind or manipulative.
