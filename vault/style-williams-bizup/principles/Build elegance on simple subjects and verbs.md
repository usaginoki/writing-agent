# Build elegance on simple subjects and verbs

**Rule:** Elegant passages combine three things: simple characters as subjects and actions as verbs; complex balance of syntax, meaning, sound and rhythm; and artfully stressed endings. Elegance sits on top of clarity, never in place of it.
**Why (reader effect):** Without the clear base, ornament is merely inflated; with it, the balance reads as grace.

## Diagnose
- Before adding any device, check that subjects are characters and verbs actions.
- Ask whether the elegance would survive if the sentence were stripped to its core; if the core is muddy, fix it first.

## Revise
1. Apply [[Character]] and [[Action]] revisions first.
2. Then, at one key sentence, add balance and a stressed ending.
3. Test: is it elegant or just inflated? "Less is more": compression is the first grace.

## Examples
> Lippmann's passage keeps *citizens* as the subject of each clause, so its balance is easy to follow.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- The most elegant elegance may be "disarming simplicity" (p. 169).
