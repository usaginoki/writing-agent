# Do not narrate your thinking

**Rule:** Don't organize a document as a story of how you thought or researched. Revise a narrative/chronological draft into a coordinate or logical structure that presents results.
**Why (reader effect):** most readers want your points, not your discovery path; chronology is easiest for the writer, not the reader.

## Diagnose
- Read the draft: is it organized as "first I... then I found..."?
- Copy each paragraph's point onto an index card; shuffle; have a reader order them. Does the reader's order differ from yours? (Exercise 8.2)

## Revise
1. Highlight points paragraph by paragraph.
2. Reorder by importance, grouping, or logic rather than time.
3. Add signals for the new order and rewrite openings accordingly.

## Examples
> ✗ "Thirty students wrote essays... In an essay before... after four weeks... in a final essay..." leading to the point last.
> ✓ point first ("They did so successfully..., but... no measurable effect"), then the chronological evidence as support.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Literary essays that explore your thinking as it happens, for patient readers.
- Legitimate narrative/cause-effect content.
