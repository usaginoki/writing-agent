# Unpack noun strings

**Rule:** Avoid long strings of nouns modifying nouns (especially invented ones or ones with nominalizations). Reverse the order of the words and connect them with prepositions; make the nominalization a verb with a character subject.
**Why (reader effect):** Noun strings feel lumpy and hide the relations between the nouns and between actions and characters.

## Diagnose
- Three or more nouns in a row; any nominalization in the string (`misdiagnosis`, `evaluation`, `review`).
- Short familiar pairs are fine (`stone wall`, `student center`, `space shuttle`).

## Revise
1. Number the words 1..n left to right.
2. Start at the right-hand head noun: turn it into a verb with a character subject.
3. Work leftward, adding prepositions (`in, of, for, to`) and reordering (5-4-3-1-2).
4. If a compound includes a technical term of your field, keep that term intact and unpack the rest (`thought disorders`).
See [[Patterns - Noun string unpacking]].

## Examples
> ✗ Early childhood thought disorder misdiagnosis often results from unfamiliarity with recent research literature.
> ✓ Physicians misdiagnose disordered thought in young children because they are unfamiliar with recent literature on the subject.

> ✗ We discussed the board candidate review meeting schedule.
> ✓ We discussed the schedule of meetings to review candidates for the board.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Established terms of art and common pairs stay.
