# Do not put a comma between two coordinated elements

**Rule (default rule with four stylistic exceptions):** Two coordinated words/phrases take no comma. Exceptions: (1) comma for dramatic contrast (short second part: *creation, and its most destructive*; *common, but not less expensive*); (2) drop the *and* and use a comma for cumulative power; (3) comma between long pairs when readers need to breathe or to sort the grammar; (4) after an introductory modifier of two coordinated clauses, comma after the modifier but none between the clauses.
**Why (reader effect):** Unneeded commas slow readers; chosen ones add emphasis or untangle structure.

## Diagnose
- Is a comma separating just two elements? Check each exception.
- If only punctuation makes a long coordination parseable, revise the sentence.

## Revise
1. Delete the comma, or keep it for an exception.
2. Long first half: comma after it (*...darkest dreams, but asserts...*).

## Examples
> ✗ ...sophisticated, and powerful ... clerical, and bookkeeping tasks.
> ✓ ...sophisticated and powerful ... clerical and bookkeeping tasks.
> ✓ Lincoln never had a formal education, never owned a large library.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- See the four exceptions; with long structures, prefer rewriting.
