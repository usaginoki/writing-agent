# Weave quotations into your own sentences

**Rule:** Integrate each quotation so it seems made for your text: say beforehand how it fits your argument, and where possible build it into your sentence's grammar rather than dropping it in. You may adjust grammar with square brackets for added/changed words and three spaced ellipsis dots for deletions (four if a whole sentence or more is deleted); never change meaning. Added emphasis requires [my emphasis].
**Why (reader effect):** A bare dropped quotation raises the suspicion that your source, not you, is doing the thinking. Weaving also pulls the quote into your own thinking.

## Diagnose
- Find every quotation: is it a sentence by itself, or lead-in with no tie to your point?
- Can you shorten it to the key words and fit it to your grammar?

## Revise
1. State your point first; then quote in support.
2. Trim to the essential words; embed in your sentence.
3. Bracket changes; use ellipses for cuts; keep the meaning.

## Examples
> Dropped: Deborah Tannen suggests ... "The increasingly adversarial spirit of our contemporary lives is fundamentally related to ... the breakdown of a sense of community."
> ✓ ...Tannen suggests that our "increasingly adversarial spirit . . . is fundamentally related" to new social developments in "the breakdown of a sense of community."

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Dropping in with a signal phrase is "acceptable but artless" (p. 233); fine when the exact wording matters.
