# Keep your voice dominant

**Rule:** Prepare readers for each quotation by saying, before it appears, how it fits your argument; make the fit seamless; prefer paraphrase and short woven quotes over long extracts. Sources support your claims; they don't replace your thinking.
**Why (reader effect):** Readers wonder "Is it you or your source that's doing all the thinking?" when quotations are dropped in bare.

## Diagnose
- Skim the paragraph: whose sentences carry the argument? Do quotations arrive without a lead-in or comment?
- Paragraphs that are mostly block quotes?

## Revise
1. Open with your claim; quote after.
2. Trim; embed; comment after if the point isn't obvious.

## Examples
> ✓ Lead-in then block: "Lipson offers this advice about paraphrase:" followed by the block.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- When the exact wording is the object of analysis (e.g., literary study), longer quotation is appropriate (inference; book does not state).
