# Be concise, not terse

**Rule:** Compress until redundancy is gone, but stop before the prose is all "gristle and bone." Let readers tell you where.
**Why (reader effect):** Readers dislike flab, but they also dislike abruptness; heavy cutting can bleach out voice and charm.

## Diagnose
- Compare versions: Strunk and White paragraph 205 words; redundancy only 139; rewording 99; to the bone 51 (a quarter of the original, but "garrulous charm" lost).
- Ask real readers whether the draft feels terse or abrupt; the writer can't tell (p. 140).

## Revise
1. First pass: erase redundancy only (word-level cuts).
2. Second pass: reword (clauses and combined sentences).
3. Third pass (optional): cut to the bone.
4. Choose the level for purpose and audience; ask readers (Exercise 9.4: 200, 150, 100, 50 words; note gains and losses).

## Examples
> 205 words: "Quite often you will discover, on examining the completed work, that there are serious flaws in the arrangement of the material, calling for transpositions."
> 51 words: "Most writers revise because few write a perfect first draft. ... if your manuscript needs surgery, it signals no weakness."

## When not to apply / exceptions
- No threshold given: "I can't tell you when you've written so concisely that your readers think you are terse." Concision alone doesn't guarantee grace.
