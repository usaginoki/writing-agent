# Keep useful nominalizations

**Rule:** Do not revise every nominalization; you cannot write well without them. Keep one when (1) it is a short subject that points back to the previous sentence; (2) it replaces an awkward `The fact that...`; (3) it names what would be the object of a verb; (4) it names a concept so familiar to readers it acts as a virtual character.
**Why (reader effect):** In these roles nominalizations aid cohesion, concreteness and concision. Judgment is needed to separate them from nominalizations that hide an action.

## Diagnose
For each nominalization ask: does it (a) sum up the preceding sentence as a short subject; (b) compress `The fact that X verbed`; (c) work as a thing (`her request`) rather than a doing (`request for assistance came...`); (d) name a concept the reader already knows (`taxation without representation`)? If none, revise it.

## Revise
- Keep (a)-(d) as is. Otherwise apply [[Turn nominalizations into verbs]].
- Test: could the noun be recast as *something she requested*? Then it is an object-naming noun (c).

## Examples
> ✓ These arguments all depend on a single unproven claim. (refers back)
> ✗ The fact that she admitted guilt impressed me. ✓ Her admission of guilt impressed me.
> ✗ There is a demand for a repeal of the inheritance tax. ✓ We demand that Congress repeal the inheritance tax.

## When not to apply / exceptions
- Familiar concepts: `abortion on demand`, `Equal Rights Amendment`, `taxation without representation` stay. Unfamiliar abstractions do not ([[Make abstractions virtual characters only when familiar]]).
- Even `request` can be an action (`Her request for assistance came after the deadline` -> `She requested assistance...`).
