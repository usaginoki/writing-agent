# Lens: Concision & shape
**Question this lens asks:** Can this sentence lose words without losing meaning, and does it reach its subject, verb and point fast and then keep a visible shape?
**Level:** sentence · **Quick mode:** CS1 (long openers), CS2 (point-first: every sentence of ~25+ words or with an introductory opener, flagged or not), CS5 (sprawl), CS8 (deadwood: tics, pairs, modifiers, categories), CS9 (metadiscourse). Skip the rest unless a signal fires.
**Mechanical hints:** LONG (>35 words) -> CS1-CS6; INTRO (long opener) -> CS1; NOM -> CS3, CS8; META -> CS9; HEDGE -> CS11 (calibrate, do not purge); INT -> CS11; NEG×n -> CS10; DNEG -> CS10 (stacked); EXPL -> CS3 and CS8 (empty subject delays the real one).
Signals only point; confirm by reading. Length alone is not the fault: sprawl is. Sentences of 20+ words are fine if shaped.

Order of work: shape first (CS1-CS7), then deadwood (CS8-CS10), then calibration (CS11), then terseness check (CS12).
When a sentence trips several checks, fix the earliest-numbered one first and re-read; later checks often disappear.
Report each edit with its check ID so the author can reject it. Cut deadwood before judging sprawl if the sentence is baggy, since cuts often fix the shape.

## Checks (in order of payoff)

### CS1 · Slow arrival at subject-verb
- **Symptom:** Main subject and verb not within ~first 7-8 words; long opener (if/since/when/although, long phrase); 15+ words before the main verb. Run the first-seven-words test.
- **Fix:** (1) Move the long opener to the end. (2) If it will not fit, make it its own sentence or independent clause joined by so/but. (3) If an opener must stay, shorten it. (4) Allow only a few long openers per text.
  - Although/since opener: drop the subordinator, put but/however (although) or so/therefore (since) before the old main clause.
- **✗/✓:** ✗ Since most undergraduates change majors at least once, first-year students who are unsure of their program should not load up their schedules... -> ✓ First-year students should not load up their schedules with requirements for one program if they are unsure of it, because most change majors at least once.
  ✗ Although some writers write well alone, most benefit from instruction -> ✓ Some writers write well alone, but most benefit from instruction.
- **Leave it when:** Opener is short (a brief if-clause, a short frame phrase). Periodic sentence is deliberate: piled-up clauses delaying a weighty closing main clause (use rarely; stress the last words). Opener carries old information that links back to the previous sentence.
- **Vault:** [[Get to the subject of the main clause quickly]] · [[Convert although and since clauses into independent clauses]] · [[First seven words test]] · [[Patterns - Revising sprawl]]

### CS2 · Point buried or dribbled
- **Symptom:** The key claim sits mid- or end-sentence after 20+ words of support; sentence "feels backward"; long abstract subject while the concrete character shows up late. Count words before the claim.
- **Fix:** (1) Identify the point. (2) Restate it as a short main clause (~8 words) with a short concrete subject and a specific verb. (3) Append support after it, often as a when/because/with clause. (4) Same logic for paragraphs: point sentence first.
- **✗/✓:** ✗ A new sales initiative that has created a close integration between the garden and home products departments has made significant improvements to the customer services Acme offers. -> ✓ Acme has significantly improved its customer services with a new sales initiative that closely integrates the garden and home products departments.
- **Leave it when:** Deliberate periodic build-up for impact; a paragraph whose logic needs a setup (motivation/problem) before the point; old information must come first to link to the prior sentence.
- **Vault:** [[Open a long sentence with its point]] · [[Open sentences with short simple elements]] · [[Point-first test]] · [[Patterns - Revising sprawl]]

### CS3 · Long abstract subject
- **Symptom:** Whole subject over 7-8 words; contains a nominalization (understanding of the drivers of...) or a relative clause (a company that focuses on... and trains...). Long-subject test.
- **Fix:** (1) Nominalization: turn it into a verb, give it a character as subject; old subject becomes a character. (2) Relative clause: convert to a when/if clause. (3) Long clause: move it after the main clause only if the main clause is short, states the point, and the moved material is newer/more complex. (4) Better: split; one sentence for the clause content, then a short subject ("Such companies...").
- **✗/✓:** ✗ Abco Inc.'s understanding of the drivers of its profitability in the Asian market helped it pursue opportunities in Africa. -> ✓ Abco Inc. was able to pursue opportunities in Africa because it understood what drove profitability in the Asian market.
- **Leave it when:** Subject is long but concrete and the reader already knows its parts (a defined term, a named standard); a technical noun phrase is the established topic; 7-8 is a rule of thumb, not a limit.
- **Vault:** [[Shorten long abstract subjects]] · [[Long subject test]] · [[Open a long sentence with its point]] · [[Patterns - Revising sprawl]]

### CS4 · Interruptions between subject-verb or verb-object
- **Symptom:** Subject, comma, long clause/phrase, comma, verb (or verb, long element, object). Reader holds breath for the verb or object.
- **Fix:** (1) Decide whether the interrupter links to the previous or next sentence. (2) Move it to the front or the end accordingly; let the next sentence pick up the last-stated element. (3) Trim wordiness in the interrupter (regarding -> about).
- **✗/✓:** ✗ Some scientists, because they write in a style that is impersonal and objective, do not easily communicate with laypeople. -> ✓ Some scientists do not easily communicate with laypeople because they write in a style that is impersonal and objective.
- **Leave it when:** Interruption is one short word or phrase (deliberately, in 2019). A short phrase may sit between verb and a much longer object (puts the long, new material last).
- **Vault:** [[Do not interrupt subject and verb]] · [[Do not interrupt verb and object]] · [[Sprawl read-aloud test]] · [[Patterns - Revising sprawl]]

### CS5 · Trailing subordinate chain (sprawl after the main clause)
- **Symptom:** Main clause followed by which/that/who stacked on which/that/who; 3+ relatives; a "which" referring to a whole clause; reading aloud brings breathlessness or stumbling. Relative pronoun trace: replace each pronoun with its noun; if unclear, it is sprawl.
- **Fix (pick one):** (1) Cut: delete who/which/that + be; that will identify -> identifying. (2) Split into sentences. (3) Turn the tail into a resumptive (repeat the noun, comma, restrictive that), summative (comma + summing noun: "a demographic event that...") or free (comma + -ing/-ed/adjective phrase on the subject) modifier. (4) Coordinate parallel items (CS6). Each modifier follows a complete segment after a comma.
- **✗/✓:** ✗ ...to less than zero, which will have serious social implications. -> ✓ ...to less than zero, a demographic event that will have serious social implications.
  ✗ ...genetic engineering, which is a new way of manipulating... units, which are the genes and chromosomes that tell... -> ✓ ...genetic engineering, a new way of manipulating... units, the genes and chromosomes that tell...
- **Leave it when:** A single restrictive relative is needed to identify the noun; chain is two short clauses that read cleanly aloud; a layered, cumulative sentence is a deliberate stylistic choice in a literary text.
- **Vault:** [[Do not chain subordinate clauses after the main clause]] · [[Extend sentences with resumptive, summative, and free modifiers]] · [[Relative pronoun trace]] · [[Patterns - Sentence-extending modifiers]] · [[Patterns - Revising sprawl]]

### CS6 · Faulty or untraceable coordination
- **Symptom:** (a) Unlike forms joined (gerund + that-clause). (b) Bare "and ... and ..." hiding cause/contrast. (c) Long element first, short last: ends abruptly. (d) Second element starts far from the start; ambiguous that/they. (e) Correlatives misplaced (not only help... but you enhance).
- **Fix:** (a) Recast one element to match the other. (b) Replace and with because/but/so to state the real relation. (c) Reorder short -> long; lengthen the last with a parallel. (d) Shorten the first half, repeat a marker word (to remember...), or repeat the noun in place of a pronoun. (e) Put each correlative member before the same part of speech. To restructure a tangled sprawl, list the parallel ideas and coordinate them.
- **✗/✓:** ✗ ...the connections between those subjective values that reflect our deepest ethical choices and objective research. -> ✓ ...the connections between objective research and those subjective values that reflect our deepest ethical choices.
  ✗ Grade inflation is a problem, and it leads to devaluation... and will not be solved by grading harder. -> ✓ Grade inflation is a problem because it devalues earned grades, but it will not be solved by grading harder.
- **Leave it when:** Careful writers accept: noun phrase + how-clause; adverb + prepositional phrase (quickly, carefully, and with help). Plain "and" is fine when no emphasis or relation needs stating. Correlatives are optional emphasis.
- **Vault:** [[Extend sentences with coordination]] · [[Order coordinate elements from short to long]] · [[Coordinate only elements parallel in grammar and sense]] · [[Keep long coordinations traceable]] · [[Use correlative conjunctions before parallel parts]] · [[Patterns - Coordination]]

### CS7 · Modifier misplacement and dangling modifiers
- **Symptom:** Opening to-infinitive or participial phrase whose implied doer differs from the main subject; an adverb or end-of-clause phrase that can attach to two things.
- **Fix:** Dangling: (1) put an explicit subject in the modifier (If developed countries are to...), or (2) make the doer the main subject (usually turns an abstract noun into an action). Ambiguous: move the modifier next to its target or to the sentence front.
- **✗/✓:** ✗ To overcome chronic poverty, a commitment to health and education is necessary. -> ✓ To overcome chronic poverty, developed countries must commit themselves to health and education.
  ✗ Overtaxing oneself in physical activity too frequently results in injury. -> ✓ Overtaxing oneself too frequently in physical activity results in injury.
- **Leave it when:** Reader cannot misattach (sentence-adverbs like "Frankly,", "To be fair,"; conventional absolute phrases); moving would break old-to-new flow with no ambiguity gained.
- **Vault:** [[Give dangling modifiers an explicit subject]] · [[Place modifiers where they are unambiguous]]

### CS8 · Deadwood inside the clause (run the pass in this order)
- **Symptom:** Content words are a minority of the sentence (book example: 9 of 34 words about the subject). Run Diagnose wordiness: 1 tics, 2 pairs, 3 implied words, 4 phrase-for-word, 5 negatives, 6 modifiers, 7 metadiscourse, 8 hedges/intensifiers.
- **Fix:**
  1. Tics: delete kind of, actually, really, particular, certain, various, individual, basically, given; test: meaning unchanged? then filler.
  2. Pairs: keep one (each and every -> each).
  3. Redundant modifiers: delete the implied one (final outcome -> outcome).
  4. Redundant categories: delete the category noun (pink in color -> pink); adjective + manner -> adverb; adjective + system/activities -> noun.
  5. Obvious implications: delete what readers infer (learn the rules for playing the game of chess -> learn the rules of chess).
  6. Phrase -> word (also turns nominalizations back to verbs): despite the fact that -> although.
  7. All-modifiers test: strike every adverb and pre-noun adjective, restore only those the reader needs.
- **✗/✓:** ✗ Productivity actually depends on certain factors that basically involve psychology more than any particular technology. -> ✓ Productivity depends on psychology more than on technology.
  ✗ If and when we can define our final aims and goals, each and every member will be ready and willing to offer aid and assistance. -> ✓ If we define our goals, we will all be ready to help.
- **Leave it when:** The "tic" is a real qualifier (generally = in most cases; virtually; certain = specific) -> keep as a hedge. Modifier restricts, distinguishes or defines (principles at war). Novice audience cannot infer the implication. Legal/ritual doublets in contracts and statutes (genre). Deliberate rhythm or emphasis by repetition. Technical terms of art that look redundant (basic research).
- **Vault:** [[Delete meaningless words]] · [[Delete redundant pairs]] · [[Delete redundant modifiers]] · [[Delete redundant categories]] · [[Delete obvious implications]] · [[Replace a phrase with a word]] · [[Delete useless adjectives and adverbs]] · [[Diagnose wordiness]] · [[Delete all modifiers test]] · [[Patterns - Meaningless words]] · [[Patterns - Redundant pairs and modifiers]] · [[Patterns - Redundant categories]] · [[Patterns - Phrases to words]]

### CS9 · Metadiscourse that announces, attributes or flags the topic
- **Symptom:** Sentence talks about the text instead of the subject: this section introduces, the first thing to say is, in this study I examine, it is important to keep in mind, it has been observed/noted/determined that, in regard to X, so far as X is concerned. Metadiscourse ratio test: count subject-matter words vs total.
- **Fix:** (1) Underline the content words. (2) Delete the rest. (3) Rebuild with the content as subject. (4) Topic flags: move X into subject position. (5) In a point/thesis sentence: delete the announcement and rewrite as a claim; if nothing or only a self-evident claim remains, the thinking is unfinished: say more.
- **✗/✓:** ✗ The last point I would like to make is that in regard to men-women relationships, it is important to keep in mind that the greatest changes have occurred in how they work together. -> ✓ The greatest changes in men-women relationships have occurred in how they work together.
  ✗ High divorce rates have been observed to occur in areas that have been determined to have low population density. -> ✓ High divorce rates occur in areas with low population density.
  ✗ In this study, I examine the history of legislation to protect children. -> ✓ Congress has legislated to protect children (self-evident: now add a real claim).
- **Leave it when:** Structural words readers navigate by (first, second, therefore, however, on the other hand). Road map in a long document (the rule targets announcements that replace the claim, not orientation). Attribution where credit, source or evidence status matters (citations, "the survey found"). Deliberate topic shift marked by "As for X". First-person intent verbs the genre expects (we argue). Warnings/instructions to readers (note that) when truly needed.
- **Vault:** [[Cut metadiscourse that announces your topic]] · [[Cut metadiscourse that attributes ideas to a source]] · [[Work topic phrases into the subject]] · [[Don't announce your point; state it]] · [[Metadiscourse ratio test]] · [[Patterns - Metadiscourse]]

### CS10 · Negatives and stacked negatives
- **Symptom:** not/no/never/none, negative prefixes, plus implicit negatives (verbs: preclude, prevent, lack, fail, doubt, reject, avoid, deny, refuse, exclude; prepositions: without, against, except; conjunctions: unless, except when). Two or more together = high risk (often with passives and nominalizations).
- **Fix:** (1) Single negative: find the affirmative word (not careful -> careless; not many -> few; not allow -> prevent; not include -> omit). (2) Stacked: work out what the sentence actually requires or permits; restate as "if you do X, do Y" or "to get Z, do X" with real people as subjects; make verbs of nominalizations, active of passives. (3) Check the meaning is unchanged (negation can carry scope: not all != none).
- **✗/✓:** ✗ Except when you have failed to submit applications without documentation, benefits will not be denied. -> ✓ To receive benefits, submit your documents.
  ✗ There is no reason not to believe that malfunctions cannot be anticipated. -> ✓ Malfunctions will surprise us.
- **Leave it when:** You want the negative emphasized (a prohibition: Never reuse a password); the negation denies a stated or expected claim (refutation: This is not a bug); the affirmative twin is not synonymous (not unusual != common; litotes as deliberate understatement); safety/legal warnings where "do not" is clearest; not-X-but-Y contrast.
- **Vault:** [[Change negatives to affirmatives]] · [[Untangle stacked negatives]] · [[Patterns - Negative to affirmative]]

### CS11 · Hedge and intensifier calibration
- **Symptom:** Too uncertain: 3+ hedges in one claim (seems, some, suggest, certain, could, possibly). Too certain: absolutes (all, every, any, completely, totally) or prove/obviously/undoubtedly/of course/it is clear that, or a claim with no hedge where evidence is not conclusive (absence of a hedge is the commonest intensifier). Reflex check: obviously/clearly openers make readers think the opposite.
- **Fix:** (1) Ask how sure you are; wording should match. (2) Cut stacked hedges to one, on the verb or quantity it governs (suggests that aspects of...). (3) Prefer suggest/indicate when confident but short of certain. (4) Delete obviously, clearly, undoubtedly, of course, it is clear that. (5) Soften absolutes: all -> many, every -> most, totally -> virtually. (6) Add one hedge where evidence is not conclusive. (7) Keep an intensifier only where it earns emphasis.
- **✗/✓:** ✗ There seems to be some evidence to suggest that certain differences could derive from influences possibly traceable to history. -> ✓ This evidence suggests that aspects of Japanese and Western rhetoric differ because of history.
  ✗ I prove that people with a gun at home are more likely to... -> ✓ My research indicates that people with a gun at home are more likely to...
- **Leave it when:** KEEP hedges in research, scientific and legal claims: they encode real uncertainty, scope and evidence strength; removing them is misrepresentation, not concision. Hedging is legitimate even in landmark claims (We wish to suggest a structure). Strip a hedge only when certainty is real (a fact, a definition, a measured result). Keep intensifiers that are literal or load-bearing (always in a specification; essential in a requirement; "do not"). Kind softening of bad news or requests is not mealy-mouthing.
- **Vault:** [[Calibrate hedges]] · [[Calibrate intensifiers]] · [[Diagnose hedge and intensifier balance]] · [[Patterns - Hedges and intensifiers]]

### CS12 · Terseness check (run last, after any cutting)
- **Symptom:** The revision reads abrupt, "all gristle and bone"; voice, connectives, hedges, or helpful signposts gone; sentences telegraphic; the draft lost 50%+ of its words.
- **Fix:** (1) Layer the cuts: redundancy only -> reword -> (optional) cut to the bone; stop at the earliest level that fits purpose and audience. (2) Restore lost voice, connectors, qualifiers. (3) Where possible, have a reader judge; the writer cannot tell. (4) Report which level was applied.
- **✗/✓:** ✗ (over-cut) Writers revise: few draft perfectly. Surgery signals no weakness. -> ✓ (redundancy-only level) Most writers revise because few write a perfect first draft; if your manuscript needs surgery, it signals no weakness.
- **Leave it when:** The genre wants compactness (abstracts, commit messages, tables, specs, UI strings, slides); the audience asked for brevity; an explicit word limit.
- **Vault:** [[Be concise, not terse]] · [[Terseness test]] · [[Reader habit checklist for shape]]

## Cheat tables

**Redundant pairs (keep one):** full and complete · true and accurate · hope and trust · hopes and desires · any and all · each and every · first and foremost · basic and fundamental · various and sundry · ready and willing · aid and assistance · final aims and goals · if and when
**Redundant modifiers (drop the first):** terrible tragedy · free gift · basic fundamentals · final outcome · true facts · past history · future plans · various different · each individual · consensus of opinion · completely revolutionize · unexpectedly surprises
**Redundant categories (drop the noun):** large in size · round in shape · honest in character · unusual in nature · of a strange type · area of mathematics · of a bright color · at an early time · in a confused state · period of time · pink in color · shiny in appearance · in an accurate manner -> accurately · educational system -> education
**Tics (delete unless they qualify):** kind of · actually · particular · really · certain · various · virtually* · individual · basically · generally* · given · practically* (*can be real hedges)

**Phrase -> word:**

|---|---|
| the reason for | why |
| despite the fact that | although / even though |
| in the event that | if |
| in a situation where | when |
| concerning the matter of; in regard to; in the area of | about / delete |
| there is a need for inspection | must inspect |
| are in a position to | can |
| it is possible that | may |
| prior to | before |
| a decrease/increase in the number of | fewer / more |
| carefully read what you wrote | edit |
| sail-powered craft turned on its side | capsized sailboat |

**Negative -> affirmative:** not careful -> careless · not many -> few · not the same -> different · not often -> rarely · not allow -> prevent · not stop -> continue · not notice -> overlook · not include -> omit · not different -> same

**Metadiscourse:**
| cut | keep |
|---|---|
| this section introduces / the first thing to say is | first, second, finally, therefore, however, on the other hand |
| in this study I examine / this paper discusses (in a point) | a roadmap in a long document |
| it is important to keep in mind that / note that (filler) | note that (a real warning) |
| it has been observed/noted/determined that | a citation or named source the reader needs |
| in regard to X / so far as X is concerned / with respect to | to sum up / candidly / I believe when stance matters |

**Hedge / intensifier ladder:**
| level | wording |
|---|---|
| too uncertain | seems... some evidence to suggest... certain... could possibly be traceable to |
| right | suggests / indicates / many / most / virtually / tends to / is likely |
| right-confident | shows (when evidence is strong) / establishes (a demonstrated result) |
| too certain | proves / obviously / undoubtedly / of course / it is clear that / all / every / totally |
Hedge items: usually, often, sometimes, almost, virtually, possibly, arguably, perhaps, apparently, somewhat, most, many, some, may, might, can, could, seem, tend, appear, suggest, indicate. Intensifier items: very, quite, rather, clearly, obviously, undoubtedly, certainly, indeed, inevitably, invariably, always, literally, key, central, crucial, fundamental, major, essential, show, prove, establish.

**Sprawl repair table:**
| problem | move |
|---|---|
| long opener | move to end, or split, or although/since -> but/so |
| long subject | nominalization -> verb; relative -> when/if; split |
| S-V or V-O interruption | move to front or end |
| point last | point first |
| relative chain | cut who/which+be; split; resumptive/summative/free; coordinate |
| long-short coordinates | reorder short -> long |
| long coordination, lost connection | shorten first half; repeat marker word or noun |

## Over-correction traps
- **Terseness:** Cutting to the bone bleaches voice and nuance. Default to the redundancy-only level; go further only when asked. Never shrink a draft to hit an arbitrary ratio.
- **Stripping hedges:** In research, legal, medical and forecasting text, hedges carry epistemic meaning (scope, confidence, evidence strength). Never delete suggests/may/most/in these samples to be "direct". If you cut one, you changed the claim. Likewise do not turn a hedge into proof/shows.
- **Inflating certainty:** Removing "seems", "approximately", or a quantifier to save two words is a content edit. Flag instead of edit.
- **Cutting signposting:** Long documents need roadmaps, headings-as-announcements, forward and back references, and first/second/finally. Cut only metadiscourse that replaces the claim or restates the obvious. Check the Metadiscourse ratio per sentence, not per document.
- **Cutting needed attribution:** Source names, citations and "according to" are evidence, not padding.
- **Negative flipping that changes meaning:** not unlikely != likely; not all != none; litotes and prohibitions often want the negative. Verify the truth conditions after every swap.
- **Defining away technical or legal terms:** doublets in contracts, term-of-art redundancy (null and void) and defined terms stay; follow the genre.
- **Forcing short sentences:** Long is fine when shaped. Do not chop a well-built coordinated or periodic sentence into fragments; splitting loses logical connectors, so keep so/but/because when splitting.
- **Resumptive/free modifier overuse:** These are repairs for sprawl, not ornaments; do not add them to sentences that already read cleanly.
- **Moving everything to the end:** End position is stress position. Do not push a trivial clause into the final slot or break an old-to-new link just to shorten the opener.
- **Deleting modifiers wholesale:** The all-modifiers test is a probe; restore every modifier that distinguishes, restricts, or defines.
- **Eliminating all the writer's voice:** Rhythm, deliberate repetition, parallel emphasis and a measured tone are choices; leave them and mention them rather than normalizing.
