# Genre adjustments
User memory (`memory/genres/<genre>.md`) overrides this file.

## Research paper / draft
- **Structure:** the introduction states a *conceptual* problem (what we don't know, then why that matters, as a larger question) and ends with the contribution. Use field conventions (IMRaD, a related-work section) rather than imposing a new structure.
- **Point early:** the abstract and introduction state the findings. Don't withhold the point.
- **Hedges carry epistemic weight.** Never remove or strengthen them without asking. Calibrate *stacked* hedges only ("it may perhaps be suggested that" → "this suggests").
- **Passive is legitimate** for methods and routine procedure. Prefer "we" for the authors' choices, arguments and contributions ([[Use I and we with metadiscourse verbs]]).
- **Terminology is fixed.** Never swap a technical term for a synonym. Define a term in the stress position the first time it appears.
- Citations, numbers, units, equations, figure and table references, and labels are untouchable.
- Signposting metadiscourse ("Section 3 shows…") is useful in long papers. Keep it brief.
- Check sources: patchwriting, quotations integrated into the writer's sentences, every borrowed idea cited (`punctuation-sources.md`).

## Essay
- An argumentative point stated by the end of the introduction, unless the essay is deliberately exploratory. If so, promise the point instead ([[Promise a withheld point at the end of the introduction]]).
- The voice belongs to the writer, so preserve stylistic quirks that memory marks as intentional.
- Elegance is welcome at the opening, at turns, and at the close.
- Keep the topic strings of the argument's characters (ideas, positions, thinkers) consistent.

## Notes (Obsidian, personal or working notes)
- Default to **quick** mode. Clarity and scannability beat polish.
- Preserve the frontmatter, `[[wikilinks]]`, tags, callouts, block references (`^id`), headings used as link targets, and list structure.
- Fragments and terse bullets are fine in notes. Don't expand them into full sentences unless asked.
- Make each note's point findable: a first line or heading that states it.
- Don't add metadiscourse or transitions to bullet lists.

## Memo / email / practical writing
- The problem is *practical* (condition + cost to *this* reader), then the request or solution. Put the point in the first paragraph.
- Name the actor and the affected reader explicitly ([[Name the affected reader and the real actor in high-stakes text]]).
- Short paragraphs. Headings or bullets for scannable steps.

## Unknown genre
Infer it from the form. If the text is over ~500 words, ask (trust off) or assume and log (trust on).
