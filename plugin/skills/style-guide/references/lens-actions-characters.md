# Lens: Actions & characters
**Question this lens asks:** Can a reader see who does what, i.e. are main characters the subjects and key actions the verbs?
**Level:** sentence (and paragraph point of view) · **Quick mode:** AC1, AC2, AC3 (first-7-words pass over flagged sentences only); AC4-AC8 in full mode.
**Mechanical hints:** NOM = nominalization candidates -> AC2/AC3 · PASS = passive candidates -> AC6/AC7 · EXPL = there is / it is...that -> AC4 · INTRO = long opener -> AC1 (verb is late) · LONG = long sentence -> AC1/AC5 (often a nominalized, scrambled sentence) · opening (first 7 words) -> AC1 (abstract subject, no verb yet). Signals are candidates, never verdicts: confirm by reading.

Core idea: readers match two levels. Grammar level (subject, verb) should carry story level (character, action). Characters-as-subjects outranks actions-as-verbs; a sentence with NO characters is the worst case.

Routine (diagnose, analyze, rewrite): (1) underline first 7-8 words (skip a 4-5 word opener); abstract subject or no verb yet = flag. (2) Ask "Who is doing what?"; list character-action pairs, bracket implied characters. (3) Verbs for actions, characters as subjects, join with because/if/when/although/how/why/whether/that.

## Checks (in order of payoff)

### AC1 · First-seven-words test (abstract subject, late verb)
- **Symptom:** In the first 7-8 words (after a short opener) the simple subject is an abstract noun (esp. a nominalization) or no verb has appeared; verb is vague (was based, occurred, means, is).
- **Fix:** 1) Name the characters (people, groups, institutions first). 2) Find the action each performs. 3) Make character the simple subject of that action's verb. 4) Link clauses (AC5). 5) If no character exists, supply one (AC4).
- **✗/✓:** The outsourcing of high-tech work to Asia by corporations means the loss of jobs for many American workers. -> Many American workers are losing their jobs because corporations are outsourcing their high-tech work to Asia.
- **Leave it when:** the subject is a short familiar concept acting as virtual character (AC3), a short back-pointing summary noun (AC2 keep-cases), or the passive is justified (AC6/AC7). Aim at "most" clauses, not all; no mechanical personification. Do not flag a sentence only because it is long.
- **Vault:** [[Make main characters subjects]] · [[First seven words test]] · [[Diagnose characters and actions]]

### AC2 · Nominalization -> verb
- **Symptom:** Action sits in a noun: -tion/-ment/-ance/-ence/-ity/-ness/-al/-ure, gerund, zero-form (request, review, result, repair), or an adjective (applicable, indicative). Worst: nominalization as subject of an empty verb; 2-3 joined by of/in/by.
- **Fix:** 1) Verbify (intention->intend, loss->lose). 2) Find who does it; make them subject. 3) Rebuild, link clauses with because/if/when/although/how/why. 4) Drop leftover articles and prepositions.
- **✗/✓:** The intention of the committee is to audit the records. -> The committee intends to audit the records.
- **Leave it when:** it fits one of four keep-cases: (a) short subject pointing back to the previous sentence ("These arguments all depend on..."); (b) replaces an awkward "The fact that..." ("Her admission of guilt impressed me"); (c) names a thing, the object of a verb ("her request", something she requested); (d) familiar concept ("taxation without representation", "Equal Rights Amendment"). Same-form words (request, review): judge by function, not suffix. Technical terms stay. Never convert every -tion word.
- **Vault:** [[Turn nominalizations into verbs]] · [[Make important actions verbs]] · [[Keep useful nominalizations]] · [[Patterns - Nominalization to verb]]

### AC3 · Empty verb + nominalization
- **Symptom:** light verb (be, seem, have, make, do, conduct, take, occur) + nominalization object: conducted an investigation, did a review, made a reduction.
- **Fix:** 1) Nominalization -> verb. 2) Replace the light verb with it (or drop it). 3) Keep the character as subject.
- **✗/✓:** The agency conducted an investigation into the matter. -> The agency investigated the matter.
- **Leave it when:** the light-verb phrase is idiomatic and shorter or clearer in context (make a decision where decide would overload the sentence), or the noun is a keep-case (AC2). A plain be linking a subject to a real description is fine.
- **Vault:** [[Replace empty verbs and there-is openings]] · [[Make important actions verbs]] · [[Patterns - Nominalization to verb]]

### AC4 · No characters / there-is frames
- **Symptom:** (EXPL) There is/was + nominalization; passive with no by-phrase and no inferable doer; "Who fears? Who recommends?" has no answer; two possible who-does-what readings.
- **Fix:** 1) Decide the real doer from context. 2) Name them as subject. 3) Generic doer: a general noun (researchers, taxpayers) beats we; one feels stiff; we is ambiguous (writer? writer+reader? everyone?); you only when addressing the reader directly. 4) Restore verb for the nominalization.
- **✗/✓:** There is no need for our further study of this problem. -> We need not study this problem further. / A decision was made in favor of doing a study. -> We decided that I should study why they disagreed.
- **Leave it when:** plain existence claims with no hidden action ("There is a bridge at the end of the road"); deliberate, harmless impersonality (AC7); the doer is unknowable and the writer must not invent one. Never invent a doer the text does not support: flag it as a question instead.
- **Vault:** [[Name characters in subjects instead of deleting them]] · [[Replace empty verbs and there-is openings]] · [[Patterns - Hidden characters]]

### AC5 · Hidden characters (possessive, of/by, adjective)
- **Symptom:** Subject is not a character but one hides in a possessive (the Wolf's jump), a by/of object (outsourcing by corporations), a noun-derived adjective (governmental, theological), or is implied.
- **Fix:** 1) List character + action pairs (governmental intervention -> government intervenes; market evolution -> markets evolve); bracket implied ones. 2) Promote each character to subject of its verb. 3) Reassemble with when/if/although/because/how/why.
- **✗/✓:** Governmental intervention in fast-changing technologies has led to the distortion of market evolution. -> When a government intervenes in fast-changing technologies, it distorts how markets evolve.
- **Leave it when:** the abstraction is what the reader needs and is familiar (AC8); the character is already a short subject elsewhere; promoting it would change meaning. Check that the rewrite means what a careful reader took the original to mean.
- **Vault:** [[Recover characters hidden in possessives, prepositions and adjectives]] · [[Diagnose hidden characters]] · [[Patterns - Hidden characters]]

### AC6 · Clause connectors and sequence (after AC2-AC5)
- **Symptom:** chains of of/in/by/on the part of between abstractions; several character-action pairs with no stated relation; events listed out of real order (number them: 4-5-2-1-3 = scrambled).
- **Fix:** 1) Name the relation: cause (because, since, when); condition (if, provided that); concession (although, though, unless); addition/contrast/result (and, but, so); embedded (that, how, why, whether). 2) Template: X because Y / Since X, Y / If X, then Y / Although X, Y. 3) Order clauses by real sequence; earlier events subordinated; result or decision last.
- **✗/✓:** Our loss in sales was a result of their expansion of outlets. -> We lost sales because they expanded outlets.
- **Leave it when:** the relation is not actually known (do not assert cause or concession the source does not claim; a bare "and" is honest); old-before-new flow or emphasis (cohesion lens) justifies a different order; the original already links clearly. Do not paste conjunctions on to fake coherence.
- **Vault:** [[Link revised clauses with precise connectors]] · [[Restore the real sequence of actions]] · [[Patterns - Clause connectors]]

### AC7 · Passive vs active (three questions)
- **Symptom:** (PASS) be + past participle, goal in subject, agent in by-phrase or dropped. Also: active in form but flat (Problem management requires cost control: abstract subject, nominalized actions).
- **Fix:** Ask for each: (1) must readers know who did it? -> active, name the doer. (2) Does the other voice give a short familiar subject first and heavy new material last? (3) Does it keep one consistent point of view/subject across sentences? If none favors passive, use active with a character subject. Metadiscourse verbs (argue, show, define, claim) -> active I/we. Flat active -> rewrite with a character.
- **✗/✓:** Research demonstrating the soundness of our reasoning and the need for action supported this decision. -> This decision was supported by research demonstrating the soundness of our reasoning and the need for action. / The terms must be defined. -> We must define the terms.
- **Leave it when (keep the passive):** agent unknown, self-evident or irrelevant (The president was reelected with 54% of the vote; records should be kept in a safe); passive opens with familiar information and delays a long new element; it keeps the paragraph's subject steady; research procedure anyone could do (preparations were added; accepted scientific usage even if it dangles an opener). Keep active I/we out of routine lab narration. Convert only when the agent matters; where hiding the agent deflects blame, name the actor.
- **Vault:** [[Choose active or passive by three questions]] · [[Use the passive when the agent is unknown or irrelevant]] · [[Use the passive to put familiar information first]] · [[Use I and we with metadiscourse verbs]] · [[Active passive choice test]] · [[Patterns - Active and passive choice]]

### AC8 · Abstractions as characters; noun strings
- **Symptom:** (a) An unfamiliar abstraction is subject amid other nominalizations (prospective intention, cognitive function...). (b) Run of 3+ nouns modifying nouns, esp. with a nominalization or an invented combo.
- **Fix:** (a) Familiar abstraction: keep as consistent subject of a series of verbs. Unfamiliar: retell with human characters (we, researchers), keep the term but strip surrounding nominalizations. (b) Number words 1..n; make the right-hand head noun a verb with a character; work leftward adding prepositions (in, of, for, to), reorder (5-4-3-1-2); keep field terms intact.
- **✗/✓:** Early childhood thought disorder misdiagnosis... -> Physicians misdiagnose disordered thought in young children... / the board candidate review meeting schedule -> the schedule of meetings to review candidates for the board
- **Leave it when:** familiar short pairs and terms of art (stone wall, student center, space shuttle, thought disorders); the audience knows the abstraction and it drives several consecutive verbs (studies should look for...); unpacking would add more words than clarity. When reading a hard text, retell it as character-action pairs rather than editing.
- **Vault:** [[Make abstractions virtual characters only when familiar]] · [[Unpack noun strings]] · [[Noun string test]] · [[Patterns - Noun string unpacking]]

## Procedure per flagged sentence
1. First 7-8 words: character subject? action verb? Pass = leave it.
2. Else list character-action pairs ("Who is doing what?"); look in possessives, of/by, adjectives.
3. Check AC2/AC3/AC4 shapes; apply verbify -> character subject -> connector.
4. Check the keep-cases and AC7 before committing. Compare meaning with the original; if the rewrite adds a claim or a doer, mark it as an editor's question.
5. Read the result in its paragraph: subject of next sentence should still be familiar (cohesion lens owns this).

## Rewrite patterns (cheat table)
| shape before | shape after |
|---|---|
| Nominalization + is/has (The intention of X is to...) | X intends to... |
| Light verb + nominalization (conducted an investigation into) | verb + object (investigated) |
| N1 was a result of N2 (Our loss was a result of their expansion) | We lost... because they expanded... |
| There is/are + nominalization | Character + verb (We need not study...) |
| N of N of N chain (a review of the evolution of the brain) | We reviewed how the brain evolved |
| Possessive + nominalization (the Wolf's jump) | Character + verb (the Wolf jumped) |
| Adjective + nominalization (governmental intervention) | Noun character + verb (a government intervenes) |
| N by/of Character (outsourcing by corporations) | Character verbs (corporations outsource) |
| No character (A decision was made to...) | Named doer (We decided...) |
| The fact that X verbed... as subject | Nominalization of the action (Her admission of guilt...) |
| Long new subject ... object = prior topic | Passive: familiar subject first, new material after by |
| It is argued / must be defined (writer's own act) | We argue / We must define |
| Noun string N1 N2 N3 N4 | Head noun as verb + prepositions (reorder right-to-left) |
| Scrambled events | Verbs in real order; earlier events in When/If clauses; result last |

## Over-correction traps
- Converting every nominalization or every passive: four nominalization keep-cases and three passive-justifying questions exist; unflagged context (previous sentence) decides.
- Treating -tion as the test: judge by function (request as an object, "her admission" replacing "the fact that"); same-form words slip through form-based scans, and some -tion words are field terms.
- Inventing characters: when the doer is unknown, irrelevant, or not in the text, do not make up a we/researchers; ask, or keep the passive.
- Forcing human subjects where a familiar abstraction already works as a consistent, verb-rich subject (studies should look...).
- Banning I/we or banning passive wholesale; or the reverse, putting I/we on routine procedures (I added preparations).
- Chopping into short all-verb Dick-and-Jane sentences; rewrite should stay as complex as the content needs, no more.
- Adding connectors (because, although) that assert relations the source never stated; changing meaning while chasing form.
- Ignoring paragraph level: fixing a passive to active can break old-before-new flow or shift point of view mid-paragraph.
- Using mechanical hints as verdicts: NOM/PASS/EXPL flags are candidates; many are fine. Also do not "fix" a passive that hides blame without naming the actor if the text is high-stakes: name the actor.
- Unpacking established noun compounds or terms of art; or breaking a technical term while unpacking the rest.
