# Lens: Elegance (grace)

**Question:** Does the one sentence that matters most end with weight and balance, or does it just trail off?

## When to apply
- Sparingly. Only at key moments: the opening point, the end of a section, the conclusion, the one claim the reader must remember.
- Only after clarity is achieved (characters as subjects, actions as verbs, concision, cohesion). Ornament on a muddy core is inflation. Test: strip the sentence to its core; if the core is not clear, fix that first. [[Build elegance on simple subjects and verbs]]
- NEVER in quick mode unless the user asks for it.
- Budget: at most 1-2 devices per page/section. When in doubt, cut: "less is more." [[Use elegant devices sparingly]]
- Default state of the text is bald clarity. Dryness is an acceptable risk.
- Skip entirely for reports, emails, docs, specs, changelogs, instructions. Rhetorical genres (speeches, closing lines, essays) may take more.

## Checks

### EL1 Weak sentence ending
- **Symptom:** an important sentence ends on a preposition, pronoun, vague adverb, or old information (`give support to`, `deal with it`).
- **Device:** end on a weighty word: noun > adjective > preposition; strongest is a nominalization or a coordinate pair of nouns.
- **✗→✓:** `projects he was willing to give support to` -> `projects he was willing to support`; `until the New World rescues us` -> `...steps forth to the rescue and the liberation of the old`.
- **Leave it when:** plainness is the goal; the sentence is not a key one; the ending already carries new information.
- **Links:** [[End sentences on weighty words]] · [[Diagnose weak sentence endings]] · [[Stress]]

### EL2 Balance of coordinated parts
- **Symptom:** two logically parallel parts built differently, so the sentence lurches.
- **Device:** give parts the same shape, matching topics, paired meanings; put the longer/heavier part second.
- **✗→✓:** `for an administration to be arbitrary against a revolutionary opposition irreconcilably opposed to it` -> `for the administration to be arbitrary and for the opposition to be revolutionary and irreconcilable`.
- **Leave it when:** the parts are not truly parallel in meaning; symmetry would distort content; it is not a main point.
- **Links:** [[Balance coordinated parts of a sentence]] · [[Coordinate only elements parallel in grammar and sense]] · [[Order coordinate elements from short to long]]

### EL3 Balance of uncoordinated parts
- **Symptom:** a long sentence whose opening and close are mirror ideas but share no shape.
- **Device:** shape subject against object, or subordinate against main clause alike (same length/phrase type, paired word meanings); heavier member second.
- **✗→✓:** `...I would not write short books on minor subjects for small audiences` after `Were I trading scholarly principles for financial security,` (short / minor / small).
- **Leave it when:** no real mirror in meaning exists; key moments only.
- **Links:** [[Balance uncoordinated parts of a sentence]] · [[Parallelism]]

### EL4 Light run-up, heavy close (of + weighty word)
- **Symptom:** a closing flat noun string (`executive governmental powers`).
- **Device:** head noun + `of` + weighty noun last.
- **✗→✓:** `all executive governmental powers` -> `all the executive powers of government`.
- **Leave it when:** the register is plain/technical; elsewhere `of` chains are nominalization sprawl. Use at endings only.
- **Links:** [[End with of plus a weighty word]] · [[Nominalization]]

### EL5 Echoing salience
- **Symptom:** the closing point restates the opening term in different, flat words.
- **Device:** reintroduce the key term (or a sound/meaning relative) in the stress position; voice reversal works (`shapes` / `is shaped by`).
- **✗→✓:** `manner is linked to matter; style affects substance` -> `style shapes, and in turn is shaped by, substance`.
- **Leave it when:** it would read cute; the term is not the section's key claim.
- **Links:** [[Echo an earlier word at the sentence end]]

### EL6 Chiasmus (rare)
- **Symptom:** two parallel halves in the same order (1A1B : 2A2B) where a mirror would close the thought.
- **Device:** reverse the second half (1A1B : 2B2A).
- **✗→✓:** `improve our thinking and our readers' understanding` -> `improve not only our own thinking but the understanding of our readers`.
- **Leave it when:** nearly always. Offer only for a truly special closing line; never in routine edits.
- **Links:** [[Use chiasmus for special effect]]

### EL7 Suspended climax (very rare)
- **Symptom:** the sentence that ends an argument is point-first and flat, when the point deserves a build-up.
- **Device:** parallel `if/when` openers, longest member last, ideally ending on `of` + nominalization.
- **✗→✓:** `Journalists should be more responsible.` -> `If journalists held themselves accountable...; if they considered...; they would make journalism more useful, public life stronger, and themselves far more worthy of esteem.`
- **Leave it when:** anything but a major conclusion. Impact is inversely proportional to frequency. This reverses point-first advice, so keep it to one per document at most.
- **Links:** [[Suspend the climax with parallel openers]]

### EL8 Sentence length at the extremes
- **Symptom:** nearly all sentences under ~15 words, or all over ~30.
- **Device:** otherwise leave length alone (clarity edits vary it). If flagged, combine or split on purpose: short for urgency/certainty, long controlled coordination for sinuous effect.
- **✗→✓:** a run of 8-word sentences -> merge two with coordination, keep one short punch for the point.
- **Leave it when:** lengths already vary; the uniformity is intentional.
- **Links:** [[Vary sentence length only at the extremes]] · [[Sentence length check]] · [[Sprawl]]

## Procedure
1. Confirm clarity passes are done and the mode is not quick.
2. Pick at most 2-3 key sentences (opening point, section end, conclusion).
3. Run [[Diagnose weak sentence endings]] on them; apply EL1 first (the cheapest, safest gain).
4. Add one balance or echo device only if the content is truly parallel (EL2-EL5).
5. Re-read: does the sentence survive reduction to a clear core? If not, revert.
6. Never change meaning, hedges or claims to get a rhythm.

## Over-use traps
- More than one or two flourishes per page; every paragraph closing on a balanced pair.
- Four devices stacked in one paragraph (balance + chiasmus + suspension + echo): "merely clever".
- Forcing symmetry that distorts meaning or pads with filler words.
- Nominalizing subjects for rhythm (`of` plus weighty word belongs at endings only).
- Elegance on top of abstractions and passives: that is inflation. [[Do not inflate prose to seem deep]]
- Imitating extravagant models (Oates, Turner, Mailer) as a house style.
- Rewriting the user's own voice into mine; keep their register. If they are plain, stay plain.
- Applying devices in technical, legal, instructional prose.
- Trying to be elegant when the text is dry but clear; dry is fine.
